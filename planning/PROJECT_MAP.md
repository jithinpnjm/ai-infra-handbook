# Project map: AI Infrastructure Handbook (start here in any new chat or with any LLM)

**Goal.** A single, original-prose, basics-to-advanced book for a DevOps engineer (strong in Linux and networking, wants to refresh from basics) moving into AI infrastructure and AI-factory platform roles (NVIDIA, CoreWeave, Nebius, DGX Cloud style). Private now, publishable later (so everything must be original wording, original diagrams, with sources credited). Docusaurus site in this repo.

## Structure is generated, and `structure.txt` is the authority
The complete book structure (20 parts, 384 chapters) lives in `planning/structure.txt`. `python3 tools/scaffold/generate.py` creates `docs/part-NN-slug/NN-slug.md` scaffolds, category files, `docs/index.md`, `planning/STRUCTURE.md` (readable index) and `planning/STATUS.md`. It never overwrites a chapter that is no longer `status: scaffold`. To add, rename or reorder chapters: edit `structure.txt`, rerun the generator. Chapter ids (for example 6.12) come from STRUCTURE.md and supersede the numbers used in older CURRICULUM_MAP revisions; use CURRICULUM_MAP for the reasoning and detail, STRUCTURE.md for numbering. When a chapter is written, change its frontmatter `status:` from `scaffold` to `drafted` (this protects it from regeneration) and update STATUS.md.

## Read these files, in this order
1. `planning/HANDOFF_PROMPT.md`: the working rules, quality bar, template, how to resume (paste its prompt into a new chat).
2. `planning/CURRICULUM_MAP.md`: the chapter plan. Later revisions override earlier text (Revision 5 adds the KAI Scheduler series 7.7a-i): Revision 2 (deeper, interview-driven), 3 (HPC track, BCM, Redfish, SuperPOD, complete Linux and networking), 4 (MLOps tools, inference engines, schedulers, operators, ASP capture).
3. `planning/STATUS.md`: what is drafted, reviewed or done.
4. `planning/INTERVIEW_TOPICS.md`: mandatory scope in priority tiers (Tier 1 first).
5. `planning/JD_MAPPING.md`: job-description requirements mapped to chapters and portfolio labs.
6. `planning/NVIDIA_DEVOPS_INVENTORY.md`: every topic of the author's existing `nvidia-devops` repo and its home here, plus known gaps.
7. `planning/COVERAGE_PROCESS.md` and `planning/coverage/`: heading-level coverage tracking. `ai-systems-performance-engineering.md` (550 rows) is the model; create one per source before writing a chapter.
8. `planning/CORPUS_MANIFEST.md`: all 71 corpus entries and how to rebuild them.
9. `planning/BACKLOG.md`: out-of-scope ideas parked for later.

## Tiers and writing order
Tier 0 = foundations (Linux, networking, Ansible from zero, Kubernetes internals; write first), Tier 1 = job- and interview-critical (Slurm, GPU Kubernetes, KAI, virtualization, IB and NCCL, inference, MLOps tools), Tier 1b = BCM, Redfish, SuperPOD, Tier 2 = senior-loop depth, Tier 3 = breadth. See `HANDOFF_PROMPT.md` (Foundations rule) and `STATUS.md`.
Source audit: `python3 tools/coverage/map_sources.py` writes `planning/coverage/maps/<book>.md` and `planning/coverage/MASTER_MAP.md` (unmapped source chapters). Deliberate skips: `planning/coverage/skips.txt`.

## Migration of the author's nvidia-devops repo
Goal: archive that repo. `planning/MIGRATION_MAP.md` (generated by `tools/migration/inventory.py`) maps every one of its 452 docs files to handbook chapters and tracks migration status; `planning/MIGRATION_ASSETS.md` covers images, data, labs and interactive pages and holds the archive checklist. Always read the mapped files when writing a chapter.

## Non-negotiables (summary; full text in HANDOFF_PROMPT.md)
- Original writing only; credit sources; no copied passages or figures.
- Depth matches the best sources: split chapters instead of compressing; 6–10k words per file.
- **AI Systems Performance Engineering is the author's top source: capture all of it** (coverage file, every section covered or explicitly moved).
- Slurm, InfiniBand, NCCL, Kubernetes GPU (including multi-node), SR-IOV/vGPU/MIG, MLOps tools, inference engines, BCM, Redfish, SuperPOD are Tier 1 or 1b.
- Every chapter: basics → intermediate → advanced, large Mermaid diagrams (click-to-zoom is built in), failure modes, a lab with venue (Mac, GCP/GKE, Nebius GPU), checkpoint questions with answers, sources, and flags for facts to verify.
- Labs are marked `untested` until actually run.
- Keep chat answers short; the depth belongs in the chapter files.
