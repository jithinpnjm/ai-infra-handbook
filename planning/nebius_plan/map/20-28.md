# 20.28 The project deep dive and the final round
Words: ~9,000 | Read time: ~60 min (full chapter; the checker code in section 3 can be skipped at ~10 min) | File: docs/part-20-nebius-sre-interview/28-the-project-deep-dive-and-the-final-round.md
One-line purpose: Prepares one project for a final team-leader hour and a possible long deep dive: picking the project, filling a one-page dossier, answering five levels of "why", owning trade-offs and mistakes, and giving answers on "why Nebius", questions to ask and the first 90 days.
Beginner friendly: partly. There is no Linux or Kubernetes depth here, but the reader must already have a real project to talk about. If you have none, use your own project work (for example the trading-bot and alert-router projects named in the chapter) and read 20.27 (post-mortems) first for the incident-story habits.
Nebius rounds served: R4 (main: the one-hour final meeting with a team leader, and the 90-minute deep dive if it happens). R3 and R2 only indirectly (the "why" ladder reuses system-design trade-offs).
Chapter verdict: MUST-READ-FULL. Nebius officially confirms only a one-hour final meeting with a team leader; the 90-minute project deep dive is one unverified secondary source. Section 3's checker is optional, but the dossier template it checks is the work product you need.

Hands-on to do:
1. Pick three candidate projects. Score each as yes or no on the six tests in section 2. Keep the ones with at least five yeses.
2. Copy the template: `cp code/part20/c28_dossier_template.md dossier.md`. Run `python3 -I code/part20/c28_dossier_check.py dossier.md`. Expect many `placeholder` lines and exit 1 (the chapter does not state the exact counts, so treat the output as your baseline).
3. Half-fill one decision with one alternative and the vague reason "much faster". Run the checker. Expect `decision_no_alternatives`, `entry_incomplete` and `unquantified`. Then fix each one with a real number.
4. Fill the dossier from your real numbers, leaving a blank where you have no figure. Run the checker until it prints `summary: clean`. Clean means complete, not correct.
5. Rehearse with a phone timer: the two-minute walk-through, then the decisions and failures in ten minutes. Then have a friend read you the five-level ladder (section 14), and answer each level with a number or an honest "I did not measure that."

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- "Walk me through the project in two minutes. What was the hardest decision?"
- "You said you moved the alerting to a new design. What did you consider and reject, what broke afterwards, and what would you do differently?"
- "Why did you choose X over Y? How did you know it would work?"
- "Tell me about something that went wrong. Why did detection take so long?"
- "Why Nebius, why SRE, and why inference?"
- "What would you do in your first 90 days on the team?"
