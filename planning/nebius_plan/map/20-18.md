# 20.18 How LLM serving fails

One-line purpose: A failure catalogue for LLM serving on vLLM: each failure gets a symptom, confirmation, "mitigate now", and "fix later", covering queue growth, KV-cache exhaustion, head-of-line blocking, OOM, hangs and Xid errors, cold start, probes, retry amplification, noisy tenants, quality drift and version skew, with a decision tree and a tested classifier.
Beginner friendly: no on first read. It assumes Linux process and log reading (`dmesg`, `kubectl describe pod`), Kubernetes probes, Prometheus names and basic vLLM serving. Read first: Part 15 chapter 01 (anatomy of an inference request) and 02 (KV cache), Part 15 chapter 16 (production serving and reliability), Part 17 chapter 02 (Prometheus), and Part 5 on probes. Then 20.14 section 5 (probes).

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- "Your vLLM endpoint's p99 time to first token tripled in ten minutes. GPU utilisation looks fine. Walk me through what you check and what you do before you know the cause." (Expect: check who is hurt, read waiting, running, KV usage and token rate, mitigate by shedding or scaling, then root cause; say that high waiting with low KV means replicas, not memory.)
- "Pods keep restarting and nobody changed anything. What is your process?" (Expect: restart reason and last state first, then liveness versus startup OOM versus runtime OOM, whether tokens were flowing before the restart, and the cost of a restart.)
- "Capacity is lower than it should be but nothing is alerting. How do you find it?" (Expect: silent shrink, compare ready with desired and the router's backend list, re-register or replace, add a ready-versus-desired alert and X+1.)
- "Users say answers are cut off, but latency and errors are fine. What do you check?" (Expect: finish_reason shares against a baseline (length, repetition), then the last config change (engine version, quantisation, speculation, sampling defaults); roll back on a canary pool.)
- "Half your replicas die and the rest of the system collapses. Why, and how do you stop it?" (Expect: the retry arithmetic, the 1.875 example, then retry budget, jitter, circuit breaker, edge shedding with 503 and back-off.)
- "Requests are queued, the token rate is zero, and `/health` says 200. What is happening and what do you do?" (Expect: a hang, not a crash; the health route reads a crash flag; capture logs and a stack dump, check dmesg for Xid, drain and restart.)
