# 1.10 Devices, udev, sysfs and procfs
Words: ~12,400 | Read time: ~82 min | File: /Users/jithinpjoseph/Documents/GitHub/ai-infra-handbook/docs/part-01-linux/10-devices-udev-sysfs-and-procfs.md

One-line purpose: Explains the three kernel windows (/dev device files, /sys device model, /proc state) and udev, then applies them to GPU, NIC and PCIe topology, device passthrough and node inventory.
Beginner friendly: no, not directly. The chapter header says "Intermediate". Read 1.3 (files and inodes), 1.8 (processes and /proc basics) and 1.12 (disks and filesystems) first. The sysfs and udev sections are the hardest part for a beginner.
Nebius rounds served: R1 (practical Linux console tasks: device names, sysctl, hardware inventory), R2 (GPU node troubleshooting: missing GPU, udev, device passthrough to containers), R4 (GPU and NIC topology as a deep-dive talking point)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read in full: section 1 (device files), section 4 (sysfs, the attribute table), section 5 (procfs and sysctl persistence), section 6 (udev, persistent names only in depth), section 8 (inventory and `nvidia-smi topo -m`), section 10 (AI patterns) and "How it fails". Skim the rules-syntax details in section 6 and the SCSI stack in section 7. Skip the udev-vs-systemd `.link` and hwdb subsection, the VFIO bind walkthrough details, and the legacy console, parallel-port and audio material.

Hands-on to do:
- `ls -l /dev/null /dev/zero /dev/random /dev/urandom /dev/sda* /dev/nvme*`, then `stat -c '%F major=%t minor=%T %n' /dev/null`.
- sysctl persistence: `sudo sysctl -w vm.swappiness=10`, then write `/etc/sysctl.d/99-lab.conf`, run `sudo sysctl --system`, and reverse it.
- udev drill: write `KERNEL=="loop0", SYMLINK+="mylab-loop"` to `/etc/udev/rules.d/90-lab.rules`, run `udevadm control --reload` and `trigger --name-match=loop0`, then `udevadm test /sys/block/loop0`. Explain why `trigger` was needed.
- Inventory: `lsblk -o NAME,MAJ:MIN,SIZE,TYPE,MODEL,MOUNTPOINT`, `lspci -nn | head`, `lscpu | grep -E 'NUMA|Socket'`, `sudo dmidecode -t system | head`, `sudo dmesg --level=err,warn | tail`.
- GPU VM only: `nvidia-smi topo -m`, and the PCIe loop that prints `numa_node`, `current_link_speed` and `current_link_width` for each NVIDIA device.

**Review notes on this chapter (for the reader):**
- Checkpoint 17 says `NODE` "crosses NUMA nodes within a socket" and `PHB` "crosses a PCIe host bridge within one NUMA node". Section 8 says `NODE` is "across the interconnect within a NUMA node" and `PHB` is "a route via the CPU". The two descriptions do not match. Check NVIDIA's `nvidia-smi topo` legend before you quote either in an interview.
- The chapter presents the `nvidia-smi topo -m` legend and the `current_link_speed/width` comparison as labelled inferences, not verified output. Confirm on a real node.
