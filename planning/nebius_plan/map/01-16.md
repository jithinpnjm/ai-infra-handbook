# 1.16 Logging, time synchronisation, cron and batch jobs

One-line purpose: Covers the three services a node needs to be debuggable and schedulable: logging (syslog, journal, logrotate, central collection), time (clocks, NTP with chrony, why skew breaks auth) and scheduled work (cron pitfalls, `at`, timers).
Beginner friendly: partly. The opening framing, the syslog severity table and the cron field table are beginner-friendly. chrony tracking output and the cron-versus-timer comparison need 1.15 (timers) and 1.8 (signals, processes) first.
Nebius rounds served: R1 (`journalctl` queries, reading logs after a failure, cron "did not run" troubleshooting, clock checks), R2 (GPU node log pipeline, Xid and kernel log triage, Munge and Slurm clock failures, health-check cron patterns), R3 (fleet time design, central logging design, log volume and retention), R4 (how you handled logs and scheduled jobs on your own project)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read in full: section 2 (the question-to-command table and GPU reading habits), section 3 "Rotating logs" (open-file/inode paragraph and `copytruncate` vs `SIGHUP`), section 4 "Central logging" (GPU log sources, alerting), section 6 "Time" (clocks, `chronyc tracking` and `sources`, the cluster-time failure list), section 7 "Scheduling recurring tasks: cron" (environment pitfall, safe-cron pattern, checklist), and "How it fails". Skim the rsyslog rule grammar, time zone details, the ntpd recipe and the `at` commands. Skip section 5 (logging philosophy) beyond one read, the ntpd section, and the Sources list.

Hands-on to do:
- `journalctl --list-boots | tail -3; journalctl -b -p err --no-pager | tail; journalctl -k -g 'Linux version' --no-pager`. Explain each result.
- `timedatectl` and `chronyc tracking` (after `apt install chrony` on a VM). Read the offset line and the `Leap status` line.
- Add a cron line in `/etc/cron.d` with `flock -n` and `timeout 60` wrapped around a command, then check it with `journalctl -u cron --since -5min` (lab step E).
- Run the `logrotate` drill (lab step C): set `size 1k`, write 60 lines with `logger`, then run `sudo logrotate -d` and `sudo logrotate -f` and list the `lab-local3.log*` files.
