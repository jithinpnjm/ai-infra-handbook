# 1.15 systemd: units, targets, timers, journald
Words: ~14,300 | Read time: ~95 min | File: /Users/jithinpjoseph/Documents/GitHub/ai-infra-handbook/docs/part-01-linux/15-systemd-units-targets-timers-journald.md

One-line purpose: Teaches systemd from unit files up (types, dependencies, drop-ins, targets, timers, slices, sandboxing, journald) and applies them to ordering and protecting daemons on a GPU node.
Beginner friendly: partly. Sections 1 to 3 (what systemd is, unit types, `systemctl` commands) are readable cold. Dependencies (section 4), resource control (section 10) and sandboxing (section 11) assume you know processes, cgroups and environments. Read 1.14 section 6 (init and PID 1) and 1.8 (processes, signals) first.
Nebius rounds served: R1 (`systemctl status`, `journalctl`, failed units, boot-slow diagnosis, `status=203/EXEC`), R2 (GPU daemon ordering, `kubelet`/`containerd` after Fabric Manager, health-gate units, `Delegate=yes`, cgroup driver alignment, node-level timers), R3 (node protection design: slices, OOM, `MemoryLow` vs `MemoryMax`), R4 (how you managed services and logs on your own project)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read in full: section 2 "Service types" and "Common [Service] options" table, section 3 (`systemctl` commands and "Enabling and starting"), section 4 "Dependencies and ordering" (the Wants/After rule and the GPU drop-in example), section 5 (drop-ins and the `ExecStart=` reset), section 10 "Protecting the host" bullets, section 12 (`journalctl` queries and persistence), section 14 (status codes), section 16 (GPU patterns), and "How it fails". Skim the unit-type table, the targets table and the sandboxing table. Skip the `networkd`, `resolved` and `timesyncd` details, the SysV runlevel material in section 15, and the Sources list.

Hands-on to do:
- `ps -p 1 -o comm=; systemctl get-default; systemctl list-units --type=service --state=failed; systemd-analyze blame | head; systemd-analyze critical-chain`. Explain what the critical chain shows on your own VM.
- Write the `hello.service` demo (lab step B): `enable --now`, `kill -9` the main PID, then watch `Restart=on-failure` bring it back. Read `journalctl -u hello`.
- Run `sudo systemctl edit hello` to add `MemoryMax=50M` and `CPUQuota=20%`, then `systemctl cat hello` and `systemctl show hello -p MemoryMax,CPUQuota` (lab step C).
- Run `journalctl -b -p warning --no-pager | tail -20` and `journalctl --disk-usage`. Then check `Storage` in journald config and whether `/var/log/journal` exists.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
1. A GPU node came back after a reboot and `kubelet` runs before the NVLink Fabric Manager is ready. How do you fix the ordering without editing vendor units?
2. What is the difference between `Wants=`, `Requires=` and `After=`? Give a case where a failure should not stop your unit.
3. A training job exhausted memory and `sshd` and `kubelet` went down with it. What systemd settings would have prevented this, and what would you avoid?
4. A service keeps restarting and then stops with "start-limit-hit". What do you check, and how do you reset it?
5. A cron job you expected never ran. How would you find it, and how would you rebuild it as a timer?
6. After a crash the journal from before the crash is gone. Why, and what do you change?
