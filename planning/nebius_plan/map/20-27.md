# 20.27 Post-mortems, on-call and incident leadership
Words: ~10,200 | Read time: ~70 min (full chapter; the Python code blocks can be skipped at ~12 min) | File: docs/part-20-nebius-sre-interview/27-post-mortems-on-call-and-incident-leadership.md
One-line purpose: Teaches how to tell a past incident as a short report with numbers, how incident roles and handover work, how to write a blameless post-mortem with checkable action items, and how to talk about toil, on-call load and error budgets.
Beginner friendly: partly. The content is behavioural and needs no deep Linux, but it assumes you have led or seen an incident. Read 20.26 first (the live incident drill), then 17.11 (incident response) for the severity vocabulary.
Nebius rounds served: R4 (team lead and deep-dive questions about an incident you led), R2 (behavioural follow-up during troubleshooting), R3 (SLO and error-budget reasoning in system design). Nebius has not published its on-call, severity or post-mortem practice.
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read core ideas 1, 2, 4 and 5 and the eight "How to answer out loud" prompts in full, because they are what R4 asks. Skim 3, 8 and 9 for numbers only, and skip the linter and timeline code except to know what they check.

Hands-on to do:
1. `cd code/part20 && python3 -m pytest -q test_c27_postmortem_lint.py test_c27_timeline_builder.py` (the chapter says 24 pass). Not required for the interview, but it shows you the rules work.
2. `python3 c27_postmortem_lint.py c27_pm_good.md` (expect 0 findings), then `python3 c27_postmortem_lint.py c27_pm_bad.md` (expect 15 findings and exit 1). Read the rule codes; they are the checklist for action items.
3. `python3 c27_timeline_builder.py c27_notes.txt` (expect detect 19 min, mitigate 40 min, resolve 113 min). Then change one tag and see the error.
4. Write your own real incident in the notes format, build the timeline, write the post-mortem from the section list, and lint it. Then say the three-minute answer aloud with a timer. (The trading-bot incidents in your own project are real candidates for this; see the report.)

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- "Tell me about the worst incident you have led. What was the impact, what did you decide, and what is different now?"
- "Tell me about a time you made a mistake in production. Was anyone blamed?"
- "Walk me through your post-mortem. What happened to the action items?"
- "How do you decide the severity of an incident, and when do you declare one?"
- "What counts as toil, and how do you decide whether to automate it?"
- "A product team wants to ship on Friday, and the error budget is nearly gone. What do you do?"
