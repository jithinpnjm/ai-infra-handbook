# 2.10 HTTP, TLS and PKI basics
Words: ~9530 | Read time: ~64 min | File: docs/part-02-networking/10-http-tls-and-pki-basics.md
One-line purpose: Covers the HTTP exchange and its status codes, what TLS 1.3 negotiates (SNI, ALPN, forward secrecy, mTLS), how certificate chains and trust stores decide whether a client connects, and the Kubernetes certificate-expiry and inference-gateway failure modes.
Beginner friendly: partly. HTTP methods, status codes, curl timing and the certificate error table are beginner-reachable. The crypto primitives and handshake are intermediate; read 2.6 (TCP round trips) and 2.8 (DNS and SNI names) first, and treat section 2 as vocabulary, not something to master.
Nebius rounds served: R1 (openssl s_client, curl -v and -w timing, SNI vs SAN, status codes), R2 (expired cert or missing intermediate outages, 502/503/504 on inference endpoints, idle-timeout resets), R3 (certificate rotation and gateway design), R4 (how your project's endpoints and mTLS were set up)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read 1 ("HTTP in practice" table and status-code paragraph, "Connections and versions" for idle timeouts and streaming, "`curl` as a diagnostic tool"), 2 (only the primitives table and the forward-secrecy paragraph), 3 (TLS 1.3 handshake, SNI, ALPN, mTLS, the openssl command block), 4 (certificate fields, chain, trust stores, CAs, revocation and expiry alerts, the private-CA lab block), 5 (kubeadm expiry, cert-manager, containerd trust, clock), 6 (troubleshooting table), 7 (lab steps 1, 3, 4 and the Python lab) and exercises 1, 4, 5. SKIM "Reading a protocol trace", the metadata-privacy paragraphs in section 2, and the ECH and onion-routing detail. Reason: certificate and endpoint failures are the most likely R2 scenario, but the cryptographic background is deeper than an SRE interview needs.

Hands-on to do:
- Read a live chain: `openssl s_client -connect <your-endpoint>:443 -servername <name> -showcerts </dev/null`, then pipe the leaf into `openssl x509 -noout -subject -issuer -dates -ext subjectAltName`.
- Time a request: `curl -sS -o /dev/null -w 'dns=%{time_namelookup} tcp=%{time_connect} tls=%{time_appconnect} ttfb=%{time_starttransfer} total=%{time_total}\n' https://<endpoint>/`. Name the slowest stage.
- Test a backend by IP with the right name: `curl --resolve <name>:443:<backend-ip> https://<name>/`, and compare it with `curl -k` to show why -k hides the problem.
- Expiry check: `openssl x509 -in cert.pem -noout -checkend 2592000` (exit code non-zero if it expires within 30 days).
- Lab steps 2 to 4: build a private CA and SAN certificate with the openssl block in section 4, serve it with nginx or `openssl s_server -www`, then break it with a missing SAN and a missing intermediate. Python lab: `python3 code/part02/http_tls_pki_lab.py`.
