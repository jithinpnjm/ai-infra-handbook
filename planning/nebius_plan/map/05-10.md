# 5.10 Services, endpoints and kube-proxy: the life of a Service
Words: 14,500 | Read time: 97 | File: docs/part-05-kubernetes/10-services-endpoints-and-kube-proxy-the-life-of-a-service.md
One-line purpose: How a Service's stable ClusterIP becomes a real pod IP in the kernel (kube-proxy in iptables, IPVS, nftables or eBPF modes), how EndpointSlices track ready backends, and how to debug Services layer by layer.
Beginner friendly: no for the packet path. Read 5.3 and 5.8 first, and Part 2 on NAT, conntrack and iptables (2.23). The types table, the DNS names and the readiness section are readable on their own.

Hands-on to do:
- Watch Endpoints change: `kubectl get endpointslices -l kubernetes.io/service-name=<svc> -o wide` and `kubectl get endpoints <svc> --watch`; fail a readiness probe on one pod and see the address drop.
- Debug from a client pod: `kubectl run -it --rm dbg --image=nicolaka/netshoot --restart=Never -- bash`, then `curl -v http://<svc>.<ns>.svc.cluster.local/health`, `curl -v http://<clusterIP>:80/` (bypasses DNS), and `curl -v http://<podIP>:8000/health` (bypasses the Service).
- On a node (iptables mode): `iptables-save -t nat | grep -E "KUBE-SVC|<svc>" | head -20`, then `conntrack -L -d <clusterIP> | head`.
- On a node (IPVS mode): `ipvsadm -Ln | grep -A4 <clusterIP>`; for nftables: `nft list ruleset | grep -A3 <clusterIP>`.
- Offline: `python3 code/part05/kube_proxy_service_sim_lab.py` (iptables probabilities, EndpointSlice sharding, traffic-policy simulator).
