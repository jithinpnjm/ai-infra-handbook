# 1.23 Performance methodology: USE, workload characterisation, drill-down
Words: 14958 | Read time: 100 | File: /Users/jithinpjoseph/Documents/GitHub/ai-infra-handbook/docs/part-01-linux/23-performance-methodology-use-workload-characterisation-drill-down.md

One-line purpose: Teaches a repeatable performance method (problem statement, USE, RED, workload characterisation, drill-down, queueing and scalability maths) and applies it to Linux hosts and GPU clusters.

Beginner friendly: partly. The ideas are plain but dense, and the chapter assumes processes, memory, disks and the observability commands from 1.8, 1.12, 1.17 and 1.18. Read sections 1 to 6 first; the queueing and scalability maths in section 9 can wait for a second pass.

Nebius rounds served: R1 (60-second Linux checklist, troubleshooting method), R2 (GPU node and service troubleshooting), R3 (capacity estimation, Little's law, queueing, percentiles, availability trade-offs), R4 (goodput, MFU and how you measured a win)

Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS (3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14, 17, 18) - the method, the 60-second checklist, USE/RED and the queueing and capacity sections are what the interviewers test; the time-scale and vocabulary material can be skimmed, and the checkpoint list repeats the body.

Hands-on to do:
- Run the 60-second checklist on any Linux VM: `uptime; dmesg -T | tail; vmstat -SM 1 5; mpstat -P ALL 1 3; pidstat 1 3; iostat -sxz 1 3; free -m; sar -n DEV 1 3; sar -n TCP,ETCP 1 3; top`.
- Fault injection (lab step 2): `stress-ng --cpu 1` pinned to one core, then `fio --rw=randread --bs=4k --iodepth=32`, then `tc qdisc add dev eth0 root netem delay 50ms loss 1%`. Name each fault from the checklist output only.
- Queueing check: run a small HTTP server with a 10 ms delay under load at several rates, plot mean and p99 against utilisation, and compare with the M/D/1 curve (r = s(2 - rho)/(2(1 - rho))).
- GPU node (if available): `nvidia-smi dmon -s pucvmet -d 1` and `nvidia-smi topo -m` during a matmul loop, then during an idle loop, and explain why GPU-Util alone misleads.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- A training job is 30% slower than last week. What do you do in the first 15 minutes?
- GPU-Util shows 100% but throughput is low. Why, and what do you measure instead?
- Explain USE and RED. When do you use each, and how do they complement each other?
- Explain Little's law and apply it to an LLM serving endpoint. How many requests are in flight at 200 req/s and 50 ms?
- Why would you not run a serving cluster at 95% utilisation?
- Scaling from 8 to 64 GPUs gives only 3x. What are your hypotheses and tests?
