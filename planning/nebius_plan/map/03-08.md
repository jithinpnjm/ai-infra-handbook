# 3.8 Building CLIs, agents and exporters
Words: ~11450 | Read time: ~76 min (full read) | File: docs/part-03-python/08-building-clis-agents-and-exporters-prometheus-exporters-redfish-client.md
One-line purpose: Shows how to build operational tooling that is safe at fleet scale: nine design principles, argparse/Click/Typer CLIs, resilient HTTP clients, Prometheus exporters (custom collector, cardinality), a Redfish BMC client, GPU host collectors, node agents under systemd, packaging, profiling, and a GPU diagnostics CLI capstone.
Beginner friendly: partly. Assumes 3.1 to 3.7 and 2.31 (monitoring). The principles, CLI basics, retries and exporter-metric rules are approachable. The Redfish resource tree and the Practical MLOps case study need the Part 7 BMC background that the book points to.
Nebius rounds served: R1 (writing small CLIs and exporters, exit codes, subprocess and config), R2 (node agents under systemd, drain and fleet tooling, Prometheus metrics, debugging a tool), R3 (fleet-scale fan-out and failure isolation as design input), R4 (own project's tooling and operational design)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. A rusty Senior SRE can skip the Click/Typer walkthroughs, the Practical MLOps case study, the packaging comparison details and the book-style asides. Read the nine principles, argparse, timeouts and retries, pagination completeness, Prometheus metric types, naming and cardinality, the custom-collector pattern, the Redfish session and task pattern, the node-agent lifecycle and systemd limits, and the common-mistakes and interview lists (about 40 min of the 76).
