Hands-on to do:
- On a 3-node kind or kubeadm cluster: `kubectl cordon <node>`, then `kubectl drain <node> --ignore-daemonsets --delete-emptydir-data --timeout=5m`, then `kubectl uncordon <node>`. Run it once with a PodDisruptionBudget that blocks the drain, and read the error.
- Run `kubectl get pods -A -o wide --field-selector spec.nodeName=<node>` before and after a drain to see what moved.
- Run the chapter's Python lab (`code/part05/day2_node_lifecycle_sim_lab.py`) and change one threshold (for example, the 50-worker etcd rule) to see the output change.
- Exercise 1 in section 9: join a worker with a `validating` taint, run a validation Job, remove the taint, then cordon, drain and uncordon while a Deployment with a PDB keeps running.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- Managed or self-managed Kubernetes for a GPU training platform: how do you decide?
- One big cluster or several? Where do the boundaries go, and what do you give up?
- Walk me through safely maintaining 500 GPU nodes, covering patching, drains and validation.
- A drain on a GPU node is hanging. What do you check, in what order, and what do you avoid?
- How do you handle a GPU node that falls off the bus, automatically and at 3 a.m.?
- What do you page on, and what do you send to a ticket?
