# 1.1 The Linux big picture: kernel, userspace, distributions

One-line purpose: Gives a from-zero map of a Linux system (hardware, kernel, user space, syscalls, processes, users, distros) and places GPU-node components on it so failures can be pinned to a layer.
Beginner friendly: partly. It is the entry point, so nothing has to come before it, and the analogies (office building, paused board game) help. It assumes a terminal and some comfort with "process" and "file". Beginners should run the section 5 commands while reading.
Nebius rounds served: R1, R2, R4
Chapter verdict: MUST-READ-FULL. It is the foundation for every later Linux chapter, and the layer-first troubleshooting habit is what R1 and R2 test.

Hands-on to do:
- `ps -ef --forest | head -40`, then find PID 1, three `[kthreadd]`-style kernel thread entries, and your shell's ancestors up to PID 1 (Part A).
- `strace -f -e trace=execve,clone,clone3,wait4,exit_group bash -c 'ls /tmp > /dev/null'` and mark the clone, execve, and exit lines (Part B).
- `sleep 300 &`, then `ps -o pid,ppid,stat,comm -p $!`; then `sleep 300`, Ctrl-Z, and watch the STAT letter change (Part D).
- Make a zombie with a short Python fork where the parent sleeps without waiting, then explain who reaps it (Part D, item 9).
- `uname -r`, `cat /etc/os-release`, `lsmod | head`, `dmesg | tail -20`, and `ls /dev | head` on any Linux box (sections 5.1 and 5.5).

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
1. Walk me through what happens when you type `ls` in bash and press Enter.
2. After a reboot the GPU is gone and `nvidia-smi` says it cannot talk to the driver. How do you debug it?
3. A process shows state D and `kill -9` does nothing. What is happening and what do you do?
4. A training pod disappeared with no error in its own log. What would you check?
5. What is the difference between kernel mode and root? Why does it matter for a container?
6. A binary fails with `GLIBC_2.xx not found` on one node but runs on another. Why, and how would you fix it?
