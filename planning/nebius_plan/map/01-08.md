One-line purpose: Builds the process model (fork/exec, states, zombies, load average) and the tools to observe, signal, limit and cgroup-cap processes, then applies them to hung jobs and pod shutdown.

Hands-on to do:
- `ps -o pid,ppid,stat,etime,cmd -p $$`, then `sleep 300 &` and `jobs -l`, `ps -o pid,ppid,pgid,sid,tty,stat,cmd -p $!`.
- Zombie drill: start a Python parent that forks a child which exits without `wait()`, find it with `ps axo stat,pid,ppid,comm | awk '$1 ~ /^Z/'`, then kill the parent and watch the zombie disappear.
- Signal drill: a bash script with `trap ... TERM`, `trap ... EXIT`; send `kill -USR1`, then `kill -TERM`, then `kill -9` and explain why the EXIT trap did not run.
- Deleted-file drill: `dd` a 200 MB file, hold it with `tail -f`, `rm` it, confirm `df` does not drop, then `sudo lsof +L1` and kill the holder.
- Pressure drill: `stress-ng --cpu 2 --timeout 20s &` with `vmstat 1 5` and `uptime` before and after; then `systemd-run --scope -p MemoryMax=100M` on a Python allocation of 400 MB and check the exit status and `dmesg`.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
1. A training job slowed down overnight. Walk me through how you find out whether it is CPU, memory or I/O bound.
2. A pod takes 30 seconds to terminate and then exits with code 137. What happened and how do you fix it?
3. The load average is 60 on a 16-core node and the CPUs are mostly idle. What is going on?
4. `df` says the disk is full but `du` cannot find the space. How do you find the cause?
5. A distributed job hangs with GPUs at 0%. Describe your triage from the process side.
6. Why can't `kill -9` remove a process, and what are your options when it is in state D?
