# 3.6 Concurrency: threads, processes and asyncio
Words: ~10200 | Read time: ~70 min (full read) | File: docs/part-03-python/06-concurrency-threads-processes-and-asyncio.md
One-line purpose: Explains Python's three concurrency models (threads, processes, asyncio), why the GIL decides between them, and the production patterns for fleet fan-out: bounded concurrency, backpressure, timeouts, cancellation, retries with jitter, and diagnosing hangs.
Beginner friendly: partly. Assumes 3.1 to 3.4 and the subprocess and signal basics of 3.5. The GIL and asyncio concepts are new to most people, so the Concepts and GIL sections are the entry point; the asyncio internals and the Learning-style digests are not needed to answer questions.
Nebius rounds served: R1 (Python scripting: fan-out over many hosts, timeouts, error aggregation), R2 (DevOps tooling: polling fleets, hung-process diagnosis, bounded retries), R4 (own project: concurrency model and backpressure choices, one process per GPU)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read the concepts and GIL, the model-choice table, bounded concurrency, the thread queue and futures patterns, `bounded_map`, the asyncio core concepts (timeouts, cancellation, `TaskGroup`), the worker-pool template, retries with jitter, and the pitfalls table with diagnosis tools (about 35-40 min of the 70). Skip the asyncio comprehension and async-generator details, the Learning-style asides and the Sources list.
