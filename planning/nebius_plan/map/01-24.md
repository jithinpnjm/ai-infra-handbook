# 1.24 Observability tools: perf, ftrace, BPF and bpftrace

One-line purpose: Gives the working toolkit for live Linux and GPU-host diagnosis (counters, perf profiling and tracing, Ftrace, BCC and bpftrace), with the kernel data sources and overhead rules behind each tool.

Beginner friendly: no. Level is Advanced, and it assumes 1.23 (method) plus 1.8, 1.11, 1.10, 1.17 and 1.18. Read first: 1.23 sections 5 and 6, then sections 2, 6 (perf stat, perf record, flame graphs), 8 (BCC names and bpftrace one-liners), 9 and 11. Treat Ftrace and the kernel-source detail in section 3 as reference.

Nebius rounds served: R1 (live "why is this process slow" console work), R2 (GPU host and Kubernetes node troubleshooting), R4 (how you diagnosed a real production issue)

Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS (1, 2, 6 perf stat/record/flame graph, 8 BCC table and bpftrace one-liners, 9, 10, 11, 13, 14) - these are the tools an SRE reaches for in a live incident; Ftrace (7), the kernel-source detail in section 3, and the checkpoint list can be read for recall only.

Hands-on to do:
- `sudo perf stat -a -- sleep 5` and `perf stat ./prog` (read IPC and context-switches; check whether PMCs exist in your VM).
- `sudo perf record -F 99 -a -g -- sleep 30; sudo perf report --stdio -n` and, if FlameGraph is available, `sudo perf script > out.perf && stackcollapse-perf.pl out.perf | flamegraph.pl > cpu.svg`.
- `sudo perf trace -p <PID>` on a busy process, and `sudo perf trace --duration 10 -a` for slow syscalls only.
- `sudo runqlat 10 1; sudo biolatency -m -D 10 1; sudo offcputime -p <PID> 20 > off.txt` (BCC: scheduler, disk, off-CPU) during an `fio` or `stress-ng` run.
- `sudo bpftrace -e 'tracepoint:raw_syscalls:sys_enter { @[comm] = count(); } interval:s:10 { exit(); }'` (syscalls per process, then exit).

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- Explain counters, profiling and tracing, and the overhead of each.
- What is the difference between a tracepoint, a kprobe, a uprobe and USDT? Which would you pick in production?
- A process is slow but its CPU is low. How do you find out why? (off-CPU analysis, delay accounting, perf trace, PSI)
- Walk through producing and reading a CPU flame graph. What does a missing stack mean?
- A CUDA training process has one core at 100% while its GPUs idle. What do you run, and what would you expect to see?
- How would you safely trace a production node, and what are the overhead risks of strace versus perf trace or BPF?
