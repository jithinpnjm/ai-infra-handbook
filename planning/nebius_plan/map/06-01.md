Beginner friendly: partly. Assumes Part 1 (Linux) and Part 2 (networking). Sections 1-3 use dense HPC arithmetic (pipelining, FLOPS precision, Rpeak/Rmax). Read sections 5, 6, 7 first, then come back to 2 and 3 for the MFU and Rpeak vocabulary.

Nebius rounds served: R2, R3, R4 (R1 only for the fleet-check habit in section 6).

Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS (4, 5 HA/HTC/HPC subsection, 6 invariants and fleet-CLI subsection, 7 stack) plus SKIM-FOR-RECALL for 1-3 and 8; SKIP the history and facility material. Reason: the interview cares about fault semantics, the "which layer is slow" method, and the cost-per-token framing, and the rest is HPC theory.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- If a 4,000-GPU training job slows down on its second night with no alarm, what do you check first, and why does the failure-recovery row matter?
- Explain the difference between a high-availability service cluster and an HPC training cluster when one node dies.
- What is MFU, and why would a platform team measure cost per token or tokens per watt instead of jobs completed?
- A job launches cleanly on 64 nodes but runs ten times slower. What configuration do you check first?
- How do you verify in seconds that all 500 nodes detected their full RAM after an outage?
- Why does a TOP500 rank or a headline FLOPS number not tell you how fast your training job will be?
