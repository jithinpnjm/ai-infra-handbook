One-line purpose: Explains how Slurm decides who runs next and how much each group may use: associations and limits, the priority formula, Fair Tree, backfill, preemption, and a worked multi-team GPU policy.

Beginner friendly: no for the first pass. It assumes 6.7 (accounting database and associations) and 6.8 (partitions and GPU defaults). Start with the "Why is my job pending" decision list (section 8), then backfill (section 5), then preemption for AI work. Come back to Fair Tree math later.

Nebius rounds served: R2 (job pending triage, limits and reasons), R3 (multi-team GPU policy, fairness, preemption and checkpoint design), R4 (policy governance and review cadence), R1 (sacct and sreport reporting basics).

Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS (1 accounting model and TRES billing, 2 association limits and the three-QoS pattern, 3 priority formula with weight-size gotcha, 4 Fair Tree guarantee and practical guidance, 5 backfill, 6 preemption and AI work, 7 policy design intro, 8 pending decision list) plus SKIM-FOR-RECALL for the simulator, checkpoint and sacct/sreport; SKIP sources. Reason: most of the interview on scheduling policy is about backfill accuracy, preemption mode choice for GPU training, and how to explain why a job is pending.

Hands-on to do:
- Run `python3 code/part06/fairtree_sim.py` and predict the ranking first (physics has 60 shares, alice 5000 and bob 500 usage; biology has 40 shares, carol and dave 100 each). Expected order: carol 1.00, dave 0.75, bob 0.50, alice 0.25.
- On a cluster with accounting (or a Lab A from 6.7): `sprio -l`, `sshare -al`, and `squeue -j <id> -o "%i %T %r %S"` to read the pending reason.
- Read the `squeue --start` estimate, then change one job's `--time` and see whether its start estimate moves (backfill test, chapter exercise 9.6).
- Before and after changing `bf_window` or `bf_max_job_test` in `SchedulerParameters`, capture `sdiag` cycle times (chapter exercise 9.8).

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- Explain associations, accounts, QOS and TRES. What is `billing` and why does MAX_TRES matter for GPU bills?
- A job is stuck at "Priority". What do you check, in what order?
- Why do accurate `--time` limits matter for backfill? What happens when users request the partition maximum?
- How would you set up preemption for GPU training with checkpoints, and why not SUSPEND?
- How do you give a team a guaranteed share of GPUs while letting others borrow idle capacity?
- What does Fair Tree guarantee about users in sibling accounts, and how do you pick the decay half-life?
