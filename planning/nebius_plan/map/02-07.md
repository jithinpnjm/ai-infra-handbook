# 2.7 UDP, ICMP and the diagnostic toolkit: ping, traceroute, mtr and path MTU
Words: ~8830 | Read time: ~59 min | File: docs/part-02-networking/07-udp-icmp-and-the-diagnostic-tools-ping-traceroute-mtr.md
One-line purpose: Gives the reachability toolkit (ping, traceroute, mtr, nc, tracepath) and explains UDP, ICMP and Path MTU so you can split "is it down, is the port closed, or is it a size problem" in minutes.

Hands-on to do:
- Step-down MTU test: `ping -M do -s 1472 <host> && echo ok`, then 1452, 1432, 1400 until it passes. Then run `tracepath -n <host>` and `ip route get <host>` to see any cached PMTU.
- Port versus ping: `ping -c 3 <host>`, then `nc -zv <host> 443` and `curl -v --connect-timeout 3 https://<host>/`. Note which one fails and what that tells you.
- Traceroute modes and loss: `traceroute -n 8.8.8.8`, `sudo traceroute -T -p 443 -n example.com`, `mtr -n -r -c 100 8.8.8.8`. Check whether any hop loss carries on to the final hop.
- Namespace MTU black hole (lab step 5, needs the chapter 2.5 namespaces `hA - r - hB`): `ip -n r link set rB mtu 1400`, then `ip netns exec hA ping -M do -s 1472 10.2.0.2` and read the "need frag" message. Then block ICMP 3/4 on r and watch a 10 KB TCP send hang in tcpdump.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- Walk me through how traceroute works. Why might a hop show `* * *` or high latency while the hops after it are fine?
- A large file transfer hangs, but small requests and the TCP handshake work. What do you check, and what are the fixes in order?
- Which ICMP messages should a firewall never block, and what breaks if you block them?
- What is the MTU inside a VXLAN overlay on a 1500 underlay, and on a 9000 underlay? How do you verify it?
- A ping succeeds but the application cannot connect. What do you test next, and why?
- How do you test jumbo frames end to end across a cluster?
