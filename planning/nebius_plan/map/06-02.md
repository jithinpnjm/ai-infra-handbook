One-line purpose: Gives the node roles, the separate networks, and the job path of a cluster, so you can say where a workload belongs and which layer a symptom comes from.

Beginner friendly: partly. Read 6.1 sections 5 and 7 first. The network and latency material needs Part 2 networking basics. The NUMA, RAID, and HyperThreading material is advanced for a beginner, so skip it on the first pass.

Nebius rounds served: R1 (networking, login and node roles, top-down debugging), R2 (job life cycle, prolog/epilog, Slurm to Kubernetes mapping), R3 (anatomy and failure isolation, capacity planning, cluster-size design), R4 (runbooks and documentation as team-lead practice).

Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS (2 node roles and their "must never happen" column, 3 networks and latency, 5 post-build checks and debugging direction, 6 job life cycle and hidden services, 7 scale table and HPC-to-Kubernetes mapping) plus SKIM-FOR-RECALL for 4 and 8; SKIP the storage and NUMA internals. Reason: the interview asks which workload goes on which node and how a job flows, and the Slurm-to-Kubernetes mapping is the bridge to Nebius's Soperator setup.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- Walk through the node roles and networks of a 256-GPU cluster. Why is putting an NFS server on a compute node a serious mistake?
- A user says the login node is frozen after their IDE remote session. What do you check, and what limits should exist?
- Compare raw latency and MPI latency for 1 GbE, 100 GbE, and InfiniBand. Which number decides a tightly coupled job's speed?
- Map Slurm concepts to Kubernetes: slurmctld, partition, sbatch, and environment modules.
- Walk through a batch job from submit to epilog. What happens when an epilog is missed?
- Your client cannot reach the scheduler daemon. What order do you debug in?
