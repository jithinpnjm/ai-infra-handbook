One-line purpose: Designs a loop that finds a failing GPU before the customer does (signals, Xid policy, active diagnostics, node lifecycle, blast-radius limits, spare-pool sizing, silent capacity loss, safe drain of a serving node, supply SLO, RMA), with a tested state machine, limiter, spare-pool calculator and promtool-checked alert rules.
Beginner friendly: partly. The loop and the limits are explained from first principles, but DCGM metric names, Xid codes, cordon and drain, and the Kubernetes termination order need background first. Read chapter 17.4 (DCGM exporter) and 11.4 (GPU Operator), plus the Part 5 Kubernetes chapter on nodes and drains, before section 2. Read 20.14 section 5 for probes.
Nebius rounds served: R3 (primary: design a self-healing fleet, capacity estimation, availability), R2 (Kubernetes cordon, drain, node conditions, probes and GPU alerting), R4 (deep dive on incidents and automation limits)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS (1, 3, 6, 7, 8, 9, 10, "How to answer out loud", Scenarios A and C). These carry the reasoning interviewers push on: why automation needs limits, why spares scale with repair time, and why a drained node must not kill streams. Skim 2, 4, 5, 11 and 12 for recall; the Python and rule file are for the lab only.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- "Design a system that detects a failing GPU before the customer does, using health checks, telemetry and automatic hardware replacement." (Expect: the loop in one sentence, three signal sources including the workload comparison, a decide component separate from detection, and the lifecycle with owners.)
- "Your automation just cordoned four nodes in ten minutes. Should it cordon a fifth?" (Expect: the fleet budget, one per failure domain, hourly rate, circuit breaker; a held node goes to a human and stays counted.)
- "How many spare nodes do you keep for 512 nodes at a 10 percent annual failure rate?" (Expect: average in repair is 0.42 at 72 hours, three spares for 99.9 percent; state the inputs as assumptions; a 14-day repair needs seven.)
- "Xid 79 fired on three nodes in different racks within five minutes. What do you do?" (Expect: suspect a shared cause such as a driver rollout or power event, confirm on one node, keep the freeze, drain only nodes with new evidence.)
- "How do you drain a node that is in the middle of streaming answers?" (Expect: stop new routing, wait up to a grace period set from the longest stream, evict; hard fault means fence and evict at once; timeout and escalation for a blocked drain.)
- "A node has been cordoned for nine days with no ticket. How do you find out what happened and what changes?" (Expect: check the lifecycle state, give the node an owner first, then fix the step that let it go silent, such as a failed validation that only logged.)
