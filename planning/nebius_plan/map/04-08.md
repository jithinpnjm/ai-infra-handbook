# 4.8 Ansible handlers, conditionals, loops, blocks and error handling
Words: ~10,500 | Read time: 70 min | File: docs/part-04-automation/08-ansible-handlers-conditionals-loops-blocks-and-error-handling.md
One-line purpose: Covers the control-flow tools that make a fleet change safe: handlers (and their failure traps), `when`, loops, `block`/`rescue`/`always`, failure policy, imports vs includes, and the drain-change-validate-return pattern.
Beginner friendly: partly. The ideas are explained with analogies, but the reader needs 04-06 (playbook phases, `changed`, `serial`) and 04-07 (`register`, facts) first. Read 04-06 section 1 and 04-07 section 2 before this chapter.
Nebius rounds served: R2 (primary: handlers, error handling, rolling-change design, debugging a skipped task), R4 (safe rollout and rollback policy for a fleet change, team-lead judgement), R1 (`reboot` plus `nvidia-smi` as a Linux health check)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS (1 "Handlers" incl. the "lost handler after failure" trap, 2 "Conditionals: when", 3 "Loops" for `until`/`retries` and the list-vs-loop point only, 4 "Error handling" incl. "Overriding judgement", "Blocks: rescue and always", "Stopping the play and fleet-level policies", "Retries and eventual consistency", 5 "Imports versus includes" (table only), 6 "Patterns for operational flows" incl. both subsections, 7 "Debugging control flow" table, 9, 10; checkpoint answers 1, 3, 4, 9, 10, 12, 13, 15, 16, 20). Hands-on to do:
- `python3 code/part04/ansible_control_flow_lab.py` from the handbook root (folder exists there) before touching hosts.
- Handler test: notify one handler from two tasks, confirm one restart. Then make a later task fail and confirm the handler is lost on the first run. Fix it once with `force_handlers: true` and once with `meta: flush_handlers`.
- Reproduce the string-boolean bug: run `ansible-playbook site.yml -e flag=false` with a `when: flag` task, then fix it with `when: flag | bool`.
- Block and rescue: use a fake installer script that fails on one host. Confirm the recap shows `rescued=1`, then add a `fail` in `rescue` and confirm `failed=1` and a non-zero exit code.
- Run a failing task with `ansible-playbook -vv` to see `false_condition`, or with `strategy: debug` to inspect `task_vars` and use `redo`.
- Poll a flaky port with `until`/`retries`/`delay` and compare the run time with a fixed `pause`.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
1. How do handlers work? When do they not run, and how would you guarantee a restart after a partial failure?
2. Explain `block`, `rescue` and `always`. How is a rescued failure reported, and why would you re-raise it?
3. What is the difference between `failed_when`/`changed_when` and `ignore_errors`?
4. Static versus dynamic includes: what changes for tags, `--list-tasks` and `when`?
5. Walk me through a safe GPU node maintenance playbook, from drain to return to service.
6. How do you know a GPU node is really ready after a reboot, and why must a failed node stay drained?
