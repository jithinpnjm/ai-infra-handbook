# 2.11 Switching internals, VLANs, trunking and spanning tree
Words: ~9580 | Read time: ~64 min | File: docs/part-02-networking/11-vlans-trunking-and-spanning-tree.md
One-line purpose: Explains what a switch does with a frame, how VLANs and 802.1Q tags split one physical network, how Spanning Tree stops loops (and why modern designs avoid it), and how LLDP and BFD prove cabling and fast failure detection.
Beginner friendly: partly. VLANs, access and trunk ports, and the loop symptoms are beginner-reachable. Read 2.3 (Ethernet frames, MAC learning, ARP) first. Section 1 (forwarding paths, crossbars, cut-through) is hardware depth you can skim.
Nebius rounds served: R1 (VLAN and trunk config, bridge/ip commands, STP basics, LLDP), R2 (broadcast storm and switch-CPU spike from a loop, MAC flapping, cabling checks), R3 (L2 domain sizing and why AI fabrics are routed with no STP), R4 (how your project's node networks and VLANs were laid out)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read 1 ("How a network device moves a packet" four-step path, "Cut-through versus store-and-forward, and buffers" for microbursts and incast), 2 (802.1Q tag, port types, native VLAN mismatch, "VLANs in a GPU cluster", "Linux VLANs and bridges"), 3 (loops, "How STP works", "Protection features", "Diagnosing loops", "Modern designs avoid STP"), 4 ("Detecting neighbours" for LLDP and two-way checks, and BFD), 5 (L2 design rules), 6 (lab steps 1, 2 and 4 and the Python lab) and exercises 2, 3 and 6. SKIM "Three forwarding paths", "Across the fabric: shared memory, bus, crossbar", "RSTP and MSTP" and the topology graph theory. Reason: the VLAN, loop and LLDP material is directly testable in R1 and R2, while switch-ASIC internals and graph theory are rarely asked of an SRE.

Hands-on to do:
- Software switch: create a bridge and two namespaces with veth pairs, ping across, then `bridge fdb show`. Flush the table with `bridge fdb flush` and ping again to watch MAC relearning (lab step 1).
- VLAN access ports: `ip link add br0 type bridge vlan_filtering 1`, add ports, then `bridge vlan add dev <port> vid 100 pvid untagged` for an access port and `bridge vlan add dev <trunk> vid 100` for a trunk. Check with `bridge vlan show` and `tcpdump -e -ni <trunk> vlan`.
- Deliberate loop: join two bridges with two veth links, leave STP off, send a broadcast with `arping`, and watch `ip -s link` counters climb. Then `ip link set br0 type bridge stp_state 1` and check `bridge link` for a blocking port.
- Commissioning check on any host with LLDP: `sudo lldpcli show neighbors` to see which switch port the NIC is plugged into.
- Python lab: `python3 code/part02/vlans_stp_switching_lab.py` (802.1Q TCI, STP root election, two-way hello and MTU).
