# 5.18 Scheduling in depth: kube-scheduler, affinity, taints, priority and preemption

One-line purpose: Explains how kube-scheduler places a pod (queues, filter and score, binding), the placement rules (node and pod affinity, spread, taints), priority and preemption, why default scheduling stalls distributed GPU jobs, and how Kueue, DRA and topology labels fix that.

Beginner friendly: partly. Labels, requests and limits, nodeSelector, taints and PriorityClass are learnable on a first read. The scheduling-framework internals (queues, extension points), NUMA and DRA are advanced. Read 5.3, 5.7 and 5.9 first; 5.9 supplies the requests, limits and QoS vocabulary this chapter uses throughout.

Nebius rounds served: R2 (Pending pods and FailedScheduling messages, taints, PDBs, priority and preemption; the most likely Kubernetes troubleshooting task), R3 (GPU capacity and fragmentation arithmetic; scheduler throughput at scale), R4 (how your own project places GPU jobs), R1 (only the resource and node-selection basics)

Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS (1 scheduling cycle and Filter/Score, 2 resource accounting and fragmentation, 3 all placement rules, 4 priority, preemption and PDBs, 5 the gang problem and Kueue objects, 8 debugging and the symptom table, 9 GPU platform patterns, 11, 12, exercises 1, 2, 3 and 6). Skim the DRA and NUMA material in section 6 for recall, skip the Volcano internals, Slurm integration and "extending the scheduler" in section 7 (deprecated policy ConfigMaps and custom schedulers). The reason: the FailedScheduling reading and the placement-rule semantics are the likely R2 questions, and fragmentation and gang deadlock are the likely R3 arithmetic.

Hands-on to do:
- Read a real pending pod: `kubectl describe pod <p> | sed -n '/Events/,$p'`, and split the FailedScheduling message into its per-reason counts. Then check each reason with `kubectl get nodes -L <label>` and `kubectl describe node <n> | sed -n '/Allocated resources/,/Events/p'`.
- Taint experiment: `kubectl taint nodes <n> dedicated=gpu:NoSchedule`. Start a pod with only the matching toleration, observe that it can still land on an untainted node, then add a `nodeSelector` and see the placement fixed. Remove the taint with `kubectl taint nodes <n> dedicated=gpu:NoSchedule-`.
- Priority test: create `training-low` and `inference-critical` PriorityClasses from the chapter, fill the cluster with low-priority pods, then start a high-priority pod. Watch the victims and `status.nominatedNodeName` in events.
- Spread test: deploy 6 replicas with `topologySpreadConstraints` on `kubernetes.io/hostname`, check the counts per node with `kubectl get pods -o wide`, then scale to 9 and see the skew.
- Fragmentation drill (from the chapter's lab, run locally): `python3 code/part05/gpu_scheduler_simulator_lab.py`. Check its `LeastAllocated` vs `MostAllocated` output against your hand calculation of free GPUs per node.
- Scheduler metrics: `kubectl get --raw /metrics | grep -E '^scheduler_(pending_pods|schedule_attempts_total|preemption)'`.
