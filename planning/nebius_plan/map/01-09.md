# 1.9 Shell scripting that survives production
Words: ~13,300 | Read time: ~89 min | File: /Users/jithinpjoseph/Documents/GitHub/ai-infra-handbook/docs/part-01-linux/09-shell-scripting-that-survives-production.md

One-line purpose: Teaches shell scripting from quoting and tests to the production habits (strict mode, traps, locks, retries, idempotence) and the AI-node script patterns, with a rule for when to switch to Python.
Beginner friendly: partly. Needs 1.2 (shell, redirection, pipes), 1.3 and 1.6 (permissions), and 1.8 (signals and traps) first. Quoting, word splitting and the test operators are new to most beginners, so read them slowly.
Nebius rounds served: R1 (Python scripting and shell scripting are both explicitly in R1), R2 (scripting for DevOps, health checks, entrypoints), R4 (examples for the project story)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read sections 1 (shape of a script), 2 (quoting and expansion), 3 (exit codes), 4 (tests), 5 (loops), 6 (temp files, heredocs), 8 (strict mode, traps, locks, retries), 10 (common mistakes), 12 (AI patterns) and "How it fails" in full. Sections 7 and 9 are useful for reference. Skip section 11 unless you have time, and treat the sources section as skip.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
1. Your cleanup step deleted the wrong directory. How could an unquoted or empty variable do that, and how do you prevent it?
2. A script runs fine in your terminal but fails under cron or Slurm. What do you check?
3. What is the difference between `./script.sh`, `source script.sh` and `exec ./script.sh`?
4. Explain what `set -euo pipefail` does and where `set -e` is silently ignored.
5. Write a GPU node health check that cannot hang and exits non-zero with a reason.
6. Two cron runs overlap and corrupt a file. How do you make the job single-instance?
