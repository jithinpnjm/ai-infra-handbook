# 2.9 DHCP, NAT and PAT
Words: ~8640 | Read time: ~58 min | File: docs/part-02-networking/09-dhcp-nat-and-pat.md
One-line purpose: Explains how a machine gets its address and boot settings (DHCP, DORA, relays, PXE) and how private hosts reach the outside (NAT, PAT, conntrack), with the failure modes that cause outages on GPU provisioning and egress.
Beginner friendly: partly. The DORA exchange and the PAT picture are beginner-reachable. Netfilter hooks and conntrack need 2.5 (routing, forwarding) and 2.7 (UDP) first; read 2.4 for subnets before the DHCP scope material.
Nebius rounds served: R1 (DORA, 169.254 symptom, SNAT vs DNAT vs masquerade, conntrack reading), R2 (node will not boot or PXE hangs, pods or nodes lose Internet, NAT port exhaustion, table full), R3 (provisioning design and egress capacity for a large cluster), R4 (how your project's network egress and provisioning were built)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read 1 "DORA", "Relays, scopes and options" (option table only as reference) and "PXE boot of bare-metal GPU nodes", 2 (private ranges), 3 (NAT type table and "How PAT works"), 4 (Linux NAT commands, conntrack reading, masquerade vs SNAT, hairpin, "Container and Kubernetes NAT"), 5 (NAT is not used in RDMA fabrics), 6 (troubleshooting), 7 (lab steps 2, 3, 4, 6), 9 and exercises 1, 4, 5. SKIM "Linux clients and servers", "DHCPv6, SLAAC and IPv6", "Hardening and diagnostics", "Cloud NAT" and the NAT consequences list. SKIP "Routers that run Linux: OpenWRT and BusyBox". Reason: DHCP-to-PXE and NAT-to-egress are the two real-world network-boot and egress stories for GPU clusters; the OpenWRT and home-router material is off-target.

Hands-on to do:
- Watch a lease: start dnsmasq in a namespace with a short `--dhcp-range` and `--log-dhcp`, run `dhclient -v vA` in the client namespace, and capture `tcpdump -ni <if> 'port 67 or port 68'` to see DORA (lab step 2).
- Masquerade test: `sudo iptables -t nat -A POSTROUTING -s 10.1.0.0/24 -o <outside_if> -j MASQUERADE`, then from two inside hosts connect to an outside `nc -l 80` and compare the source addresses. Check `sudo conntrack -L | head` (lab step 3).
- DNAT with a filter check: add a PREROUTING DNAT for port 8080 to the inside host's port 80, test with and without the FORWARD ACCEPT rule, and read counters with `iptables -t nat -L -v -n` (lab step 4).
- Diagnose a no-Internet host: `ip -br addr`, `ip route`, `ping <gw>`, then `sysctl net.ipv4.ip_forward`, then `sudo tcpdump -ni <outside_if> icmp or port 443` on the gateway, then `conntrack -S` and `dmesg | grep conntrack`.
- Python lab: `python3 code/part02/dhcp_nat_pat_lab.py` (T1/T2, conntrack parse, exhaustion, hairpin).
