# 6.10 Slurm GPU scheduling: GRES, NVML autodetect, MIG and device constraints

One-line purpose: Shows how a GPU becomes a schedulable Slurm resource (GRES), how a job asks for the right GPUs, how the kernel actually fences them, and how to triage the common GPU ticket (wrong GPU count, all GPUs visible, pending forever).

Beginner friendly: partly. Needs 6.8 (Gres, ConstrainDevices) first. The request-option table and the binding section are the hard parts; the isolation and troubleshooting sections are the most useful to a beginner on a GPU node.

Nebius rounds served: R1 (nvidia-smi, device files, srun checks), R2 (GPU node triage, drain on GPU fault, job-sees-wrong-GPU), R3 (GPU fleet design: whole GPU vs MIG vs MPS, billing of GPU hours), R4 (runbook ownership for GPU faults).

Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS (1 GRES and AutoDetect, 2 request options and what the job sees, 4 isolation, 5 MIG-and-choosing table, 6 GPU accounting, 7 operational drain rules and live monitoring, 8 troubleshooting table) plus SKIM-FOR-RECALL for 3, the sharing subsections MPS and sharding, the lab and the checkpoint; SKIP the sources. Reason: a GPU ticket on an Nebius-style cluster almost always lands in the isolation, request-syntax, or drain-state rows, and those are what the interviewer will ask about.

Hands-on to do:
- On a GPU node: `slurmd -C` and `slurmd -G`; then `nvidia-smi topo -m` to compare the Cores and affinity columns with what slurmd reports.
- Isolation check (chapter section 4): `srun --gres=gpu:1 bash -c 'nvidia-smi -L; ls /dev/nvidia*'` (expect one GPU), then `srun --gres=gpu:1 bash -c 'CUDA_VISIBLE_DEVICES=1 nvidia-smi -L'` and confirm no GPU outside the allocation is visible.
- Triage three commands from section 8: `sinfo -N -l`, `scontrol show nodes | grep Gres`, `srun -N 1 --gres=gpu:1 nvidia-smi -L`.
- Accounting check: `sacct -X --format=JobID,AllocTRES%60` after a GPU job, to confirm gres/gpu is recorded.
- Run `python3 code/part06/gpu_env_check_lab.py` (if the code folder is present), which needs no GPU.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- How does Slurm know how many GPUs a node has, and how do you verify it? What does `slurmd -G` show?
- What is the difference between `--gres=gpu:4`, `--gpus=4`, `--gpus-per-node=4` and `--gpus-per-task=1`? Which is best for a 2-node NCCL job?
- A user with a 1-GPU request sees all 8 GPUs. What is missing, and how do you guarantee isolation?
- A node drains with "gres/gpu count too low (0 < 8)" after a reboot. What do you check and in what order?
- Compare MIG, MPS and sharding for isolation and density. Can Slurm create MIG slices on its own?
- A multi-node PyTorch job is 25% slower than last week with the same GPUs. What scheduler-side and binding-side causes do you check?
