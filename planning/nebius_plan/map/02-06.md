# 2.6 TCP in depth: handshake, windows, congestion control, tuning and troubleshooting
Words: ~12350 | Read time: ~82 min | File: docs/part-02-networking/06-tcp-in-depth-handshake-windows-congestion-control-retransmission.md
One-line purpose: Covers how TCP builds and closes a connection, what its listen and TIME_WAIT/CLOSE_WAIT states mean in production, reliability (RTO, fast retransmit, SACK, Nagle/delayed ACK), flow vs congestion control, the bandwidth-delay product, CUBIC vs BBR, Linux tuning, reading `ss`/`tcpdump`/`nstat`, and why TCP struggles with AI incast.
Beginner friendly: partly. The handshake, the refused-vs-timed-out distinction and the state names are beginner-reachable. Windows, congestion control and the Reno/CUBIC/BBR detail are intermediate. Read 2.2 and 2.4 first; 2.5 helps for the routing side.
Nebius rounds served: R1 (TCP handshake, errors, `ss`/`tcpdump`), R2 (live troubleshooting: timeouts under load, TIME_WAIT/CLOSE_WAIT, retransmits), R3 (throughput and BDP estimation for cross-region or checkpoint transfer), R4 (own project's latency and throughput story)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read sections 1 (what transport solves, flow vs congestion control), 4 (handshake, listen queues, error table, TIME_WAIT vs CLOSE_WAIT, TCP states), 5 (RTO, fast retransmit, SACK, Nagle and delayed ACK), 6 (window, BDP table), 7 (congestion control: slow start, CUBIC vs BBR, ECN, incast), 9 (`ss`, `tcpdump`, `nstat`) and 12 (lab steps 1, 2, 7, 8, 9). SKIM sections 2, 3, 8, 10 and 11 for recall; SKIP the RINA and transport-model history and the Wireshark stream-graph detail unless asked.

Hands-on to do:
- Namespaces `c` and `s` joined by a veth pair (commands in section 12), then `sudo ip netns exec s iperf3 -s -D` and `sudo ip netns exec c iperf3 -c 10.9.0.2 -t 10 -i 1`.
- Refused vs timeout: connect to a closed port (expect fast RST, "refused"), then drop SYNs with `nft` and connect again (expect the 1, 2, 4 s SYN retries and "timed out").
- Add delay with `tc qdisc add dev vc root netem delay 50ms` and compare iperf3 with `-w 64K`, `-w 1M`, `-w 16M` against window/RTT.
- On a real host: `ss -ti dst <peer>`, `ss -s`, `nstat -az | egrep 'TcpRetransSegs|ListenOverflows|TCPTimeouts'`, and `ss -tan state time-wait | wc -l` to read the TIME_WAIT count.
- Run `python3 code/part02/tcp_in_depth_lab.py` locally (per the chapter).
