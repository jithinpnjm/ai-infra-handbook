One-line purpose: Gives the layer map (DoD/TCP-IP vs OSI), encapsulation and header overhead arithmetic (MTU, MSS, goodput, VXLAN), demultiplexing keys, and where each layer lives in Linux and AI fabrics.
Beginner friendly: partly. The analogies are good, but the history sections (ATM, RINA, circuit switching, SOS triangle) are dense for a first read. Read 2.1 first; the MTU/MSS and demux parts are the beginner-usable core.

Hands-on to do:
- `sudo tcpdump -nn -e -v -c 5 -i any 'tcp port 443'` and label each header (Ethernet, IP, TCP) and its key field.
- `ip -d link show` and `ip -s link show dev eth0`, then `ethtool -k eth0 | egrep 'segmentation|gso|tso|gro'` to see offloads.
- `nstat -az | egrep 'TcpRetrans|IpInDiscards|UdpInErrors'` and `tc -s qdisc show dev eth0`, and classify each output by layer.
- Work the 1500 MTU goodput by hand (1460 / 1538 = 94.9%) and check it against the lab; then do the MTU test `ping -M do -s 1472 <host>` and `tracepath <host>`.

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- Explain the OSI model. Where do TCP, IP, Ethernet, VXLAN and TLS sit, and what are the caveats?
- What is encapsulation? What is the overhead of VXLAN, and what happens to the MTU of the inner packets?
- How does an incoming frame find the right process on a Linux server? Walk the EtherType, protocol, and port keys.
- What is the difference between the control plane and the data plane? What still works if the routing process crashes?
- Why can't I see RDMA traffic with tcpdump, and what does that mean for debugging NCCL?
- A 9000-MTU fabric has one hop at 1500. What breaks and how do you find it?
