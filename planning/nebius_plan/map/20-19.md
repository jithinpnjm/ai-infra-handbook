# 20.19 SLIs, SLOs and alerts for token APIs
Words: ~11,950 (about 40% is YAML and Python) | Read time: 80 | File: /Users/jithinpjoseph/Documents/GitHub/ai-infra-handbook/docs/part-20-nebius-sre-interview/19-slis-slos-and-alerts-for-token-apis.md
One-line purpose: Teaches how to define good-event SLIs for streaming token APIs, turn an SLO into an error budget, and design multiwindow burn-rate pages so the on-call is woken only for user-visible burns.
Beginner friendly: partly. Read first: Part 17 chapter 01 (metrics, logs, traces) and 02 (Prometheus and PromQL basics), then 17.06 (alerting, SLOs, Alertmanager routing). Ratios and budget arithmetic are plain; the PromQL and promtool sections need 17.02. Background also from 17.03 to 17.05 (logging, GPU metrics, profiling) for the cause signals in section 7, and Part 15 chapter 14 (benchmarking, latency percentiles and goodput) for section 9.
Nebius rounds served: R3 (primary: "how will you know it is healthy", the SLO and alert answer), R2 (SRE incident and alert triage), R4 (deep-dive follow-ups on SLOs you have set)
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read sections 1, 2, 3, 5, 6, 7, 11, the "How to answer out loud" block and Scenarios 1, 2 and 4 in full; read the availability burn YAML and the calculator once; skim the TTFT and engine YAML; skip the sources and verification log.

| 6 | 6. Burn rate and the multiwindow alert | Burn rate = error ratio / (1 - SLO); the page rule needs a long window to prove the burn and a short window so the alert clears fast (14.4 over 1 h and 5 min, 6 over 6 h and 30 min, 1 over 3 d and 6 h). Symptoms, not causes, and what pages | Page only on what the user feels (burn of availability or TTFT); send causes such as GPU utilisation, KV cache, preemptions and queue depth to tickets, and keep a Watchdog heartbeat. | intermediate | "Why not alert on GPU utilisation?" and "pager too noisy" | MUST | 4.6 |

Hands-on to do:
1. `cd ai-infra-handbook/code/part20`, then `promtool check rules slo_recording_rules.yaml slo_engine_rules.yaml slo_burn_alerts_availability.yaml slo_burn_alerts_ttft.yaml slo_cause_alerts.yaml`. Needs promtool installed.
2. `promtool test rules slo_rules_test_availability.yaml` and `promtool test rules slo_rules_test_ttft_engine.yaml`.
3. `python3 -m pytest -q test_slo_burn_rate.py` and `python3 slo_burn_rate.py --slo 0.99`, then compare the 43.2-minute and 52-second figures with `--slo 0.999`.
4. Change `rate1h > 0.0144` to `> 0.0200` in `slo_burn_alerts_availability.yaml`, rerun the availability test, see the 2% case fail, then restore the file.
