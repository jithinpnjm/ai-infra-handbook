One-line purpose: A timed practice sheet with twelve system design prompts (five from a secondary source, seven added by the author), each with clarifying questions, sizing arithmetic, a 40-minute outline, graders' expectations, a failure story and curveballs, plus a rubric, a first-five-minutes script and a tested sizing calculator.
Beginner friendly: no, for first contact. Start with 20.13 (the full framework and speaking plan) and 20.12 (the sizing arithmetic), then use this sheet as the drill set. Each prompt links to the deeper chapter that it depends on.
Nebius rounds served: R3 (primary: practice for the whole system design round), R2 (prompt 5, SRE variant, and the incident questions), R4 (follow-ups on the reasoning behind any design you bring)

Likely interview questions from this chapter:
- "Design a service that serves one LLM to many customers. Traffic is bursty. One customer must not hurt another." (Expect: streaming assumptions, 90,000 tokens per second, 28 GPUs with an N+1, the 3x burst that needs 84, queue and shed and prioritise, token-based limits.)
- "How would you find a failing GPU before a customer notices?" (Expect: the three signal sources, passive against active checks, a blast-radius cap, and the detection time that must beat the 15-minute customer notice.)
- "Walk me through how you handle incidents, plan capacity and make rollouts safe." (Expect: error budget as the rollout brake, burn-rate pairs, capacity ordered against GPU lead time, mitigate before root cause.)
- "Design storage for a training cluster of thousands of GPUs. Think about throughput, checkpoints and cost." (Expect: separate bandwidth from capacity, checkpoint write burst of 16.3 GB/s for 980 GB in 60 seconds, tiers, retention.)
- "A customer's dedicated endpoint sits in one region that is short of capacity. Design for failover." (Expect: failover is the customer's design under residency, RTO and RPO first, cold against warm cost, and hysteresis against a flapping probe.)
- "Design the system that counts tokens and produces the invoice. Customers will check it." (Expect: a durable event per request, dedupe by request id, reconciliation, and the reason duplicates matter more than a small undercount.)
