# 2.3 Ethernet and L2: frames, MAC addresses, switching, ARP and NDP
Words: ~12800 | Read time: ~85 min | File: docs/part-02-networking/03-ethernet-and-l2-frames-mac-addresses-switching-arp-and-ndp.md
One-line purpose: Explains the single-link layer: MAC addresses and Ethernet frames, how a switch learns and floods, how ARP and IPv6 NDP map IPs to MACs, the Linux weak-host ARP fix for multi-NIC servers, and L2 failure diagnosis.
Beginner friendly: partly. Frames, MACs and the switch-learning story are beginner-friendly; the optics/FEC section, PAM4 and the switch ASIC internals are dense. Read 2.1 and 2.2 first.
Nebius rounds served: R1, R2
Chapter verdict: READ-SECTIONS. Read sections 2, 3, 5 (learning, flooding, loops), 6 (ARP, NDP, Linux multi-NIC ARP), 10 (failure table) and the checkpoints; SKIM-FOR-RECALL the optics names (4), Linux L2 objects (7) and AI fabric L2 (9); SKIP the Wi-Fi section (8) except the one-line CSMA/CA contrast.

Hands-on to do:
- Build the three-host bridge from section 11 step 1 in namespaces; then `sudo ip netns exec h3 tcpdump -nn -e -i e0` while `ip netns exec h1 ping -c1 10.20.0.2`, and compare `bridge fdb show br br0` before and after.
- `ip -n h1 neigh show` and `sudo ip neigh flush dev <if>` on a disposable VM, then watch ARP re-resolve with `tcpdump -nn -e arp`.
- Duplicate-address drill: add a second IP 10.20.0.2 in h3, run `arping -I e0 -c 4 10.20.0.2` from h1, and read the two MACs.
- On a real host: `ip -br link`, `ethtool -S eth0 | egrep -i 'crc|err|drop'`, `cat /sys/class/net/eth0/carrier_changes`, `sysctl net.ipv4.conf.all.arp_ignore net.ipv4.conf.all.arp_announce`.
