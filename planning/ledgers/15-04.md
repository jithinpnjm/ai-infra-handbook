## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1:** (a) Inserting `[1, 2, 3, 4, 5]`, `[1, 2, 3, 6, 7]`, and `[1, 2, 8, 9]` into `RadixCache(max_size=1000)` stores **`9` unique token slots** (sharing `[1, 2]` and `[1, 2, 3]`) instead of `14` unshared tokens, and querying `[1, 2, 3, 4, 10, 11]` returns **`match_len = 4`** (`[1, 2, 3, 4]`) with **`hit_rate = 4 / 6 = 0.6667` (`66.67%`)**; (b) when `[10, 20, 30, 80, 90, 91, 92, 93]` arrives at `max_size = 10`, leaf-first LRU evicts the older unreferenced leaf **`[40, 50]`** (`2` tokens) while keeping the shared ancestor `[10, 20, 30]` and newer leaf `[60, 70]` intact.
2. **Exercise 2:** (a) Across `100` requests with a `500`-token system prompt and `50`-token unique query, unoptimized prefill computes **`55,000` tokens**, SGLang RadixAttention computes **`5,500` tokens** (`49,500` tokens saved = **`90.0%` reduction**), and vLLM block-hash APC (`B = 16`, matching `31 * 16 = 496` tokens and missing `4` sub-block tokens per follow-up) computes **`5,896` tokens** (`396` extra tokens); (b) across `prefix_len in [0, 100, 500, 1000]`, RadixAttention cache hit rates are **`0.0%`, `66.0%`, `90.0%`, and `94.29%`**, and cold-vs-warm TTFT speedups are **`1.0x`, `3.0x`, `11.0x`, and `21.0x`**.
3. **Exercise 3:** (a) On a `1,312`-token RAG prompt (`256` sys + `4 x 256` chunks + `32` query), canonical chunk ordering `[1, 2, 5, 7]` matches **`768` prefix tokens** (`256` sys + `512` for chunks `1` and `2`), whereas reordered `[5, 7, 1, 2]` misses at chunk `5` and matches only **`256` tokens**; (b) shared document prefixes across tenants allow a timing side-channel (probing whether a document returns a fast `2.0 ms` cache-hit TTFT vs `22.0 ms` cold TTFT), which is prevented by inserting **`<id> {tenant_id}` between `<system>` and `<context>`**, while a `400%` P90 TTFT cache-thrashing spike under non-overlapping prompts at `mem_fraction_static=0.85` is remediated by setting **`--mem-fraction-static 0.92 --schedule-conservativeness 1.0`** or **`--disable-radix-cache`** (stabilizing TTFT at `22 ms`).
4. **Exercise 4:** (a) Checking `128,256` (`128K`) tokens on CPU at every decode step adds tens of milliseconds per token, whereas XGrammar precompiles context-free grammars into compressed finite-state machines with GPU bitmasks (`~1.25%` overhead) and handles context-sensitive rules via pushdown automata with tree-based stack management; (b) on the `23`-token `Person` JSON output, Jump-Forward Decoding splices the `4` deterministic strings **`'{"name": "'` (`5` tokens), `'", "age": '` (`5` tokens), `', "occupation": "'` (`6` tokens), and `'"}'` (`2` tokens)** = **`18` jumped tokens (`78.26%` of decode steps skipped)**, cutting total latency from **`690.00 ms` (naive CPU) and `279.45 ms` (FSM-only) down to `65.55 ms` (`4.26x` faster than FSM-only, `10.53x` faster than naive)**.
5. **Exercise 5:** (a) With `20.0 ms` GPU compute and `12.0 + 8.0 = 20.0 ms` CPU scheduling, serial step time is **`40.0 ms` (`50.0%` CPU overhead)** and SGLang's Zero-Overhead Scheduler overlaps Batch `N-1`/`N`/`N+1` into **`20.0 ms` (`2.0x` steady-state speedup)**, while `s.fork(4)` reduces KV tokens from **`2,800` to `1,300` (`53.57%` saved)**; (b) under `worker_match_ratios = [0.15, 0.72, 0.40, 0.10]`, `cache_aware` routing selects **Worker `1` (`0.72 >= 0.5`)** when loads are `[6, 8, 5, 12]` (`12 - 5 = 7 <= 10`), falls back to shortest-queue **Worker `0` (load `4`)** when loads are `[4, 20, 6, 8]` (`20 - 4 = 16 > 10` and `20 > 4 * 1.5`), and `--enable-dp-attention` eliminates **`8x` KV cache duplication** on a `1`-KV-head model at `TP=8` for up to **`1.9x` higher decode throughput**.
