## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (Transport Layer Problems, Marshaling Grammars, and Connection-Oriented vs. Connectionless):** (a) The 3 marshaling grammar families (`CN` Ch. 2) are fixed-length fields (TCP/IP base headers), Type-Length-Value (`TLV`, TCP options), and shared dictionaries/schemas (protobuf/gRPC, YANG); (b) flow control (`rwnd`, advertised by receiver) protects the receiving process's buffer from overflow, whereas congestion control (`cwnd`, private to sender) protects the intermediate network from congestion collapse, and the sender may have at most `min(cwnd, rwnd)` bytes in flight.
2. **Exercise 2 (TCP Handshake, Teardown, `SYN`/Accept Queues, and `TIME_WAIT` vs. `CLOSE_WAIT`):** (a) Three packets (`SYN`, `SYN-ACK`, `ACK`) are needed so both directions confirm reachability and exchange random Initial Sequence Numbers (`RFC 6528`); on the server, `tcp_max_syn_backlog` sizes the half-open SYN queue (`tcp_syncookies=1` defends against floods) and `min(backlog, net.core.somaxconn)` sizes the completed accept queue (`ListenOverflows` in `nstat`); (b) `TIME_WAIT` (`60 s` on active closer) is normal and prevents delayed duplicates, whereas accumulating `CLOSE_WAIT` on a server proves an application socket leak (peer sent `FIN`, local app never called `close()`).
3. **Exercise 3 (Reliability: Cumulative ACKs, `SACK`, Jacobson `RTO`, `RACK-TLP`, and Nagle + Delayed ACK):** (a) `compute_jacobson_rto_ms()` computes `RTO = clamp(SRTT + 4 * RTTVAR, 200 ms, 120 s)`—in a `0.2 ms` datacenter, an RTO costs `200 ms` (`1,000x` RTT) and collapses `cwnd = 1`, whereas 3 duplicate ACKs (or `RACK-TLP`) + `SACK` (`RFC 2018`) retransmit only the missing hole in 1 RTT without collapsing `cwnd`; (b) splitting a small RPC into two small `write()` calls triggers a `~40 ms` stall when **Nagle's algorithm** (waiting for an ACK before sending sub-MSS data) meets the receiver's **delayed ACK** (waiting up to `40 ms` for a second segment)—fix with `TCP_NODELAY` and coalesced writes.
4. **Exercise 4 (Bandwidth-Delay Product `BDP`, Window Scaling, and Linux Buffer Autotuning):** Using `compute_bdp_and_window_scale()` and `compute_window_limited_throughput_mbps()`: (a) a `100 Gb/s` link with `1 ms` RTT has `BDP = 12.5 MB` (needing window-scale shift `>= 8`), and a `10 Gb/s` link with `70 ms` RTT has `BDP = 87.5 MB`; (b) a `4 MB` receive window over `70 ms` RTT caps a single TCP stream at `4 MB / 0.070 s ≈ 457.1 Mb/s` regardless of NIC speed—raise `net.ipv4.tcp_rmem`/`tcp_wmem` and `net.core.rmem_max`/`wmem_max` (to `134217728` = `128 MiB`) without calling `setsockopt(SO_RCVBUF)` (which disables kernel autotuning).
5. **Exercise 5 (Congestion Control `Tahoe`, `Reno`, `CUBIC`, `BBR`, `DCTCP`, and Data-Center Incast):** Using `simulate_congestion_loss_event()` at `cwnd = 100 MSS`: on 3 duplicate ACKs, Tahoe resets `cwnd = 1`, Reno halves `cwnd = 50`, and CUBIC scales by `β = 0.7` to `cwnd = 70` (while an RTO resets all of them to `cwnd = 1`); **BBR** paces at estimated bottleneck bandwidth x min RTT without waiting for loss, and **DCTCP** uses switch **ECN** marking fractions to avoid incast buffer overflows.
6. **Exercise 6 (Diagnosing TCP Bottlenecks with `ss -ti` and `nstat`, and Comparing TCP with QUIC):** In `ss -ti`, `rwnd_limited` (or `win 0` in `tcpdump`) proves the receiving application is too slow, `sndbuf_limited` proves the sender's `tcp_wmem` is too small, and `cwnd_limited` with rising `retrans` proves network loss/congestion; **QUIC** (`RFC 9000` over UDP) combines transport + TLS 1.3 into a `1-RTT` (`0-RTT` resumption) handshake, eliminates cross-stream head-of-line blocking, and supports connection migration via Connection IDs.
