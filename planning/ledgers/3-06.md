## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (Concurrency vs. Parallelism, the GIL, and the Three Spinner Scripts, `FP` Chapter 19):** Concurrency structures overlapping tasks while parallelism executes simultaneously on multiple cores; the GIL serializes Python bytecode per process (releasing on OS I/O and C extensions like NumPy/hashlib/zlib) and switches threads every `5 ms` (`sys.getswitchinterval()`)—so replacing `sleep(3)` with `is_prime(5_000_111_000_222_021)` lets `spinner_thread.py` (via 5 ms preemption) and `spinner_proc.py` (via a separate process GIL) keep spinning while freezing `spinner_async.py` on its single-threaded cooperative event loop.
2. **Exercise 2 (Thread Synchronization, Backpressure, and `ThreadPoolExecutor` `to_do_map` + `as_completed` vs. `map`, `FP` Chapter 20):** `counter += 1` compiles into multi-step load/add/store bytecodes requiring `with threading.Lock():`; `executor.map` yields strictly in input order (stalling `proc_pool.py` on item 2 `9999999999999917`), whereas `run_threadpool_to_do_map()` pairs `executor.submit` with `as_completed(to_do_map)` to yield results out of order and isolate per-item exceptions, and `bounded_map()` uses `islice` + `wait(..., return_when=FIRST_COMPLETED)` to cap in-flight futures at `max_inflight`.
3. **Exercise 3 (Multiprocessing, Start Methods `fork` vs. `spawn` vs. `forkserver`, Pickling, and `SharedMemory`, `FP` Chapters 19 & 20):** `procs.py` achieves a `40.81s -> 10.39s` speedup across 6 cores because each worker process has its own GIL and exits on a `0` poison pill; `fork` clones only the calling thread (leaving locks held by other threads or CUDA driver contexts permanently deadlocked/corrupted in the child), whereas `spawn` starts a fresh interpreter (requiring `if __name__ == "__main__":` and picklable top-level functions), and `SharedMemory` + `gc.freeze()` share buffers without pickling or copy-on-write page faults.
4. **Exercise 4 (Asyncio Core: Event Loop, `Semaphore`, `gather` vs. `TaskGroup`, Cancellation, and `asyncio.to_thread`, `FP` Chapter 21):** `async with asyncio.Semaphore(concur_req):` throttles active coroutines, `asyncio.TaskGroup()` cancels sibling tasks on failure and raises an `ExceptionGroup` (unlike `gather`), `CancelledError` must always be re-raised after `finally:` cleanup, and `await asyncio.to_thread(save_flag, ...)` offloads blocking disk writes to a worker thread so the event loop never stalls.
5. **Exercise 5 (Asynchronous Iterators `AsyncRange`, Async Generators `multi_probe`, and PEP 530 Async Comprehensions, `LP` Ch. 20 & `FP` Ch. 21):** `AsyncRange` implements `__aiter__` and `async def __anext__` (raising `StopAsyncIteration`), `multi_probe_async_gen()` uses `async def` + `yield` over `asyncio.as_completed`, and PEP 530 allows `[x ** 2 async for x in AsyncRange(1, 5)]` and `{name: ok async for name, ok in multi_probe_async_gen(domains)}`.
6. **Exercise 6 (Production Fan-Out: Bounded Worker Pools, Full-Jitter Retries, Signal Shutdown, and Hang Diagnosis):** `run_pool()` bounds memory and concurrency via `asyncio.Queue(maxsize=queue_size)` and cancels workers cleanly after `q.join()` or `SIGTERM`; `random.uniform(0, min(cap, base * 2 ** i))` spreads retry waves with full jitter; and `py-spy dump`, `faulthandler`, `PYTHONASYNCIODEBUG=1`, and `len(os.sched_getaffinity(0))` diagnose hangs and container CPU throttling.
