## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (What Makes a Network a Fabric, Scale-Out vs. Scale-Up, and Non-Blocking vs. Non-Contending, `CN` Chapters 22 & 25):** (a) A **fabric** is defined by (1) **topological regularity** (identical, repeatable sub-topologies), (2) **scale-out growth** (adding more units of the same switch rather than replacing cores with bigger boxes), and (3) **low jitter / low queueing delay** for east-west traffic (`10:1` east-west to north-south ratio); (b) a **non-blocking** Clos has enough internal capacity (`1:1`) that no permutation of 1-to-1 flows is blocked internally, whereas a **non-contending** (scheduled/circuit) fabric prevents multiple senders from contending for the same egress port simultaneously (**incast**).
2. **Exercise 2 (3-Stage and 5-Stage Clos Sizing Math, Oversubscription, and Spine-Failure Capacity):** With `R`-port switches at `1:1` (`R/2` down, `R/2` up), a **3-stage folded Clos** has `R` leaves, `R/2` spines, and **`R^2 / 2` max endpoints** (`size_clos3_fabric(64)` -> `64` leaves, `32` spines, `2,048` GPUs), and a **5-stage Clos** supports up to **`R^3 / 4` endpoints** (`65,536` for `R = 64`); losing `1` of `S = 32` spines leaves `(32 - 1)/32 = 96.9%` capacity (`3.1%` loss, `compute_oversubscription_and_spine_loss()`).
3. **Exercise 3 (AI Back-End Rail Topologies: Rail-Optimized `ROD`, Rail-Only, Rail-Unified `RUD`, and `ToR`/`MoR`/`EoR` Racks, `ADC` Chapter 3):** In **Rail-Optimized (`ROD`)**, GPU `i` (`0..7`) on every server connects to Leaf `i` of an 8-leaf rail stripe (making same-rank GPUs across `32` servers **1 hop apart** via their shared leaf, and using `NCCL PXN` over internal NVLink to avoid 2-hop spine inter-rail traffic), typically cabled as **Middle-of-Row (`MoR`)** or **End-of-Row (`EoR`)**; **Rail-Only** omits spines between rails and routes inter-rail traffic inside servers over NVSwitch; and **Rail-Unified (`RUD`)** connects all 8 NICs of a server to a local **Top-of-Rack (`ToR`)** switch (simpler copper DAC cabling, but a single ToR failure drops all 8 GPUs of the server).
4. **Exercise 4 (Scheduled Cell-Based Fabrics with `VOQ`, Dragonfly, Torus, and Multi-Planar Scale-Out):** **Scheduled fabrics** (`Jericho3-AI`/`Ramon3`, `Silicon One`) slice packets into `64–256` B cells sprayed across all links under credit-granted fabric-wide **VOQs**; **Dragonfly** connects meshed/Clos groups with global optical links (`~3`-hop diameter, requiring non-minimal Valiant/adaptive routing); a `k`-D **Torus** has diameter `sum(n_i // 2)` (`compute_torus_diameter([4, 4, 4]) = 6` hops, ideal for nearest-neighbor communication); and **Multi-Planar** designs break out each GPU NIC across `2` or `4` independent 2-tier planes with NIC-level packet spraying and reordering.
5. **Exercise 5 (Information-Hiding Examples and Failure Domains, `CN` Chapters 19 & 20):** Bound failure domains by (1) summarizing topology at area/level boundaries, (2) aggregating reachability (`10.10.0.0/16` for `256` racks), (3) layering BGP/EVPN or SR controllers over a minimal loopback underlay, and (4) slowing state velocity with **exponential backoff** (`50, 100, 200 ms`) and **link-state flooding reduction** (`RFC 8099` / `distoptflood`).
6. **Exercise 6 (`MTBF`, `MTTR`, Availability \"Nines\", `MTBM` / `MTTI`, `SRLG`s, and the 4,096-GPU Design Calculator):** Using `compute_availability_and_downtime()`, `Availability = MTBF / (MTBF + MTTR)` where `99.99%` ("four nines") allows **`52.6` minutes/year** of downtime (`99.999%` = `5.3` min/year); eliminate hidden **SRLGs**, prefer **drain-upgrade-undrain** over risky `ISSU`, and size the 4,096-GPU 5-stage fabric (`4` blocks x `32` leaves + `4` x `32` spines + `32` super-spines at `2:1` = `288` switches, `4,096` server + `4,096` leaf-spine + `2,048` spine-superspine cables).
