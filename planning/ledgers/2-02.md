## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (Circuit Switching vs. Packet Switching, Statistical Multiplexing, and ATM's Legacy):** Circuit switching pre-reserves fixed TDM slots along a path (guaranteed bandwidth/jitter, but idle slots are wasted and central setup is slow to reroute), whereas packet switching statistically multiplexes independent header-bearing packets hop-by-hop across shared links; ATM's `53`-byte fixed cells (`5` B header + `48` B payload) died because of cell tax/padding waste, but its label-switching concept became **MPLS** and its anti-fragmentation lesson shaped Path MTU Discovery and IPv6.
2. **Exercise 2 (The Four Jobs of a Data-Carrying Protocol and the RINA Scope View):** Every data-carrying protocol performs some combination of (1) **Transport**, (2) **Multiplexing**, (3) **Error control**, and (4) **Flow/congestion control** (`CN` Ch. 3); Ethernet performs them across a single link, IP performs transport/multiplexing across an internetwork, TCP performs all four end-to-end between sockets, and RoCE v2 combines UDP (`4791`) + InfiniBand BTH transport in NIC silicon over lossless PFC/ECN Ethernet.
3. **Exercise 3 (Encapsulation Goodput Math: `1500` MTU vs. `9000` Jumbo MTU vs. VXLAN, and `100 GbE` Mpps):** Using `compute_tcp_ethernet_goodput()`: standard `1500` MTU TCP/IPv4 gives `MSS = 1460` B over `1538` wire byte-times (`94.9%` goodput), jumbo `9000` MTU gives `MSS = 8960` B over `9038` wire byte-times (`99.1%` goodput), and a `50`-byte VXLAN overlay (`14` Eth + `20` IP + `8` UDP + `8` VXLAN) reduces inner MSS to `1410` B; a minimum `64`-byte frame occupies `64 + 20 = 84` byte-times (`672` bits) on the wire, capping a `100 Gb/s` link at `148.8 Mpps` (`595.2 Mpps` on `400 Gb/s`).
4. **Exercise 4 (Demultiplexing Keys Across L2, L3, and L4):** When a NIC receives a frame, the `16`-bit Ethernet **EtherType** (`0x0800` IPv4, `0x86DD` IPv6, `0x0806` ARP, `0x8100` VLAN) selects the L3 handler, the `8`-bit IP **Protocol / Next Header** (`6` TCP, `17` UDP, `1` ICMP, `47` GRE, `50` ESP) selects the L4 transport, and the `16`-bit destination **port** (or 5-tuple, e.g., `443` HTTPS or `4791` RoCE v2) selects the socket.
5. **Exercise 5 (Data/Control/Management Planes, the Hourglass Model, and the SOS Triangle):** The **data plane** forwards packets at line rate using the FIB, the **control plane** (distance vector, link state OSPF/IS-IS, path vector BGP, or centralized IB Subnet Manager) computes loop-free paths in the RIB, and the **management plane** (SSH, NETCONF/gNMI, SNMP) configures and monitors devices; IP acts as the narrow waist of the Internet **hourglass**, and the **State / Optimization / Surface (`SOS`)** triangle states that improving path optimization (reducing *stretch*) always requires adding either more state or more interaction surfaces.
6. **Exercise 6 (Mapping the Layers to the Linux Kernel `sk_buff` Stack vs. AI Fabrics):** In the Linux kernel, packets travel as `sk_buff` descriptors through the socket layer -> TCP/UDP -> IP FIB and netfilter hooks (`PREROUTING`, `INPUT`, `FORWARD`, `OUTPUT`, `POSTROUTING`) -> egress `qdisc` (`fq_codel`, `fq`) -> NIC driver DMA rings (with `XDP` at the earliest driver hook), whereas `RoCE v2`, `InfiniBand`, and `NVLink` bypass the kernel socket/TCP/IP/`qdisc` stack via `libibverbs` / hardware DMA.
