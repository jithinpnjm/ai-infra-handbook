## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (Problem Statement, Anti-Methods vs. Scientific Method, and Time-Scale Sanity Checking):** (a) The 3 anti-methods are the *streetlight anti-method* (running familiar tools regardless of relevance), *random change* (tweaking sysctls until something changes), and *blame-someone-else* (tossing tickets without evidence); always start with the 6-question Problem Statement and the Scientific Method (Question -> Hypothesis -> Prediction -> Test -> Analysis); (b) on the time-scale table (`0.3 ns` cycle = `1 s`), DRAM (`100 ns`) is `6 minutes`, NVMe (`10–100 µs`) is `9–90 hours`, and rotational disk (`1–10 ms`) is `1–12 months`, proving that a `"10 µs"` HDD read was served from RAM cache.
2. **Exercise 2 (The Linux 60-Second Checklist and the GPU-Node USE Table):** (a) The 10 commands in `LINUX_60S_CHECKLIST` are `uptime`, `dmesg -T | tail`, `vmstat -SM 1`, `mpstat -P ALL 1`, `pidstat 1`, `iostat -sxz 1`, `free -m`, `sar -n DEV 1`, `sar -n TCP,ETCP 1`, and `top`; (b) in the USE method, check **Errors** first (objective, fast), **Saturation** second (any queueing degrades latency), and **Utilization** third, and remember that `nvidia-smi` `GPU-Util = 100%` only means at least one SM had a kernel active—check DCGM SM occupancy, tensor activity, and MFU instead.
3. **Exercise 3 (USE vs. RED, Load vs. Architecture Problems, and Five Whys):** (a) USE (Utilization, Saturation, Errors) measures hardware/software resource health, while RED (Request rate, Errors, Duration percentiles) measures user-facing service health; if Duration (`p99`) rises while Request rate is flat, it is an architecture/resource problem rather than an external load spike; (b) in Case Study 1, Five Whys traced "30% slower epochs" -> data loader blocked on NVMe reads -> NVMe read rate higher -> page-cache hit ratio dropped from `98%` to `91%` -> an uncontained prototype process consumed hundreds of GiB of host RAM.
4. **Exercise 4 (Amdahl's Law vs. Gunther's Universal Scalability Law `USL`):** (a) With serial fraction `α = 0.05` (`5%`), Amdahl's law gives `C(64) = 64 / (1 + 0.05 * 63) = 15.42x` and an asymptotic ceiling of `1 / 0.05 = 20.0x` even with infinite GPUs; (b) Gunther's USL adds the coherence term `β * N * (N - 1)`, which causes throughput to peak and then **decline** as `N` grows when cross-node communication/synchronization overhead dominates.
5. **Exercise 5 (Little's Law `L = λW` and the M/D/1 Queueing Curve):** (a) By Little's law (`L = λW`), an inference service handling `λ = 200 req/s` with average response time `W = 0.050 s` (`50 ms`) has `L = 10.0` requests in flight on average; (b) in an M/D/1 queue (`r = s(2 - ρ)/(2(1 - ρ))`), response time grows from `1.0s` at `ρ = 0` to `1.75s` at `ρ = 60%`, `3.0s` at `ρ = 80%`, and `5.5s` at `ρ = 90%`, which is why latency-sensitive services target `60%–70%` utilization rather than `95%`.
6. **Exercise 6 (Resource-Limit Capacity Extrapolation and Harmonic Mean of Rates):** (a) At `1,000 req/s` using `40%` across `16` CPUs (`640%` total CPU), each request consumes `0.64%` CPU, putting the theoretical `100%` CPU ceiling (`1,600%`) at `2,500.0 req/s`; (b) transferring `100 MB` at `50 MB/s` (`2 s`) and `700 MB` at `10 MB/s` (`70 s`) takes `72 s` for `800 MB`, giving a weighted harmonic mean of `800 / 72 = 11.1 MB/s` (not the arithmetic average `30 MB/s`).
