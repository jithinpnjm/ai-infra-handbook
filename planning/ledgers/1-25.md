## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (Casual Benchmarking, the 16 Failures, and the Bonnie++ Active-Benchmarking Investigation):** (a) *Casual benchmarking* means "you benchmark A, actually measure B, and conclude you measured C"; the *benchmark paradox* shows that requiring a product to win 3 independent 50%-chance benchmarks yields only `0.5^3 = 12.5%` probability; (b) in Gregg's Bonnie++ investigation (`739 KB/s` "per-character disk output"), `iostat` and `block:*` tracepoints showed zero disk I/O while `cachestat` and `kprobe:vfs_write` (`1.18M` 1-byte calls) proved the test was measuring 1-byte buffered writes to the page cache.
2. **Exercise 2 (The Six-Question Checklist and Instant Bandwidth Sanity-Check Arithmetic):** (a) The 6 questions (`SIX_CHECKLIST_QUESTIONS`) are: (1) Why not double? (2) Did it break limits? (3) Did it error? (4) Does it reproduce? (5) Does it matter? and (6) Did it even happen?; (b) `50,000` NFS IOPS x `8 KiB` = `400,000 KB/s` ≈ `3.28 Gbit/s`, which exceeds a `1 GbE` link (`physically_possible_on_wire = False`, proving client cache hits), and `"55 GB/s"` unidirectional on a `400 Gb/s` NIC (`400 / 8 = 50 GB/s` raw wire ceiling) is physically impossible.
3. **Exercise 3 (Storage Benchmarking with `fio`: Page Cache, Queue Depth, and SSD Steady State):** To benchmark NVMe storage honestly with `fio`, pass `--direct=1` (bypassing host RAM page cache), size the working set larger than RAM (`--size=64G`), sweep `--iodepth` and `--numjobs` (`qd=1` measures latency; `qd=64` x `8` jobs measures peak parallel IOPS), precondition SSDs past their fresh-out-of-box SLC burst into garbage-collection steady state (`--ramp_time`), and log p50/p90/p99/p99.9 latencies and thermal throttling (`--write_bw_log`).
4. **Exercise 4 (GPU Timing Pitfalls: Asynchrony, Warm-Up, Clock Locking, and Precision):** Because CUDA kernel launches are asynchronous, wall-clock timers without `torch.cuda.synchronize()` or `torch.cuda.Event` measure only CPU launch time; benchmarks must also discard initial warm-up iterations (context init, JIT, cuBLAS/cuDNN autotuning), lock or log GPU clocks/thermals (`nvidia-smi -pm 1`, `-lgc`, `clocks_throttle_reasons.active`) to avoid mistaking a hot rack (`1,410 MHz` vs `1,740 MHz`) for a software regression, and state exact precision/sparsity (`FP16`/`BF16`/`FP8`).
5. **Exercise 5 (`nccl-tests` `algbw` vs. `busbw` and Diagnosing `NET/Socket` Fallbacks):** For an AllReduce of `1 GB` in `0.010 s` across `n = 8` GPUs, `algbw = 100.0 GB/s` and `busbw = algbw * 2(n - 1)/n = 100.0 * (14/8) = 175.0 GB/s` (`compute_nccl_allreduce_busbw()`); if 2-node `all_reduce_perf` plateaus at `22 GB/s` instead of `~46 GB/s` on `400 Gb/s` NICs, check `NCCL_DEBUG=INFO` for `NET/Socket` (TCP fallback caused by a wrong `NCCL_IB_HCA`) and check `ibstat`/`perfquery` for symbol errors or link down-training.
6. **Exercise 6 (LLM Inference Open-Loop vs. Closed-Loop Benchmarking and `Median - 3*MAD` Regression Gating):** (a) A closed-loop client waits for responses before sending new requests, hiding queue growth when the server slows down (*coordinated omission*), whereas an open-loop generator sends Poisson arrivals at the target rate using realistic prompt/output length distributions to find the maximum throughput meeting p95/p99 `TTFT` and `TPOT` SLOs; (b) `evaluate_median_mad_regression_gate()` compares the candidate median of interleaved runs against `baseline_median - 3 * MAD`, preventing noisy single runs from triggering false CI failures.
