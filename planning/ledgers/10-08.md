## 6. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (Decoding Throttle Reason Bitmask):** Bitmask `0x0000000000000028` is `0x08` (`HW_SLOWDOWN`) plus `0x20` (`SW_THERMAL_SLOWDOWN`), indicating that the GPU exceeded its thermal target and internal thermal sensors triggered hardware clock reduction to prevent damage.
2. **Exercise 2 (Diagnosing the Utilization Lie):** GPU 0 with 85% SM util and 82% mem util is healthy memory-bound execution; GPU 1 with 85% SM util and 12% mem util is spinning or deadlocked in a software barrier, confirmed by querying `nvidia-smi -q -d UTILIZATION` or `nvidia-smi dmon -s um`.
3. **Exercise 3 (PyTorch Memory Headroom Calculation):** Reserved fraction is 30.2 / 40.0 = 75.5%; true available memory is (40.0 - 30.2) + (30.2 - 28.4) = 9.8 + 1.8 = 11.6 GB; a batch requiring 8.0 GB succeeds because 8.0 GB < 11.6 GB (PyTorch expands its cache by 6.2 GB using free driver memory).
4. **Exercise 4 (Thermal Headroom and Cooling Budget):** At 75°C, thermal headroom to the 85°C limit is 10°C; with cooling capacity of 3 W/°C, the GPU can absorb an additional 10°C * 3 W/°C = 30 Watts of heat dissipation before thermal throttling triggers.
5. **Exercise 5 (Persistence Mode Driver Penalty):** Persistence mode keeps the kernel driver initialized; when disabled, the driver tears down upon process exit and re-initializes on the next launch, adding a start-up delay per invocation (the notes give no figure), fixed by running `sudo nvidia-smi -pm 1` or enabling `nvidia-persistenced.service`.
6. **Exercise 6 (Clock Locking for Inference Jitter):** Run `sudo nvidia-smi -lgc 1410,1410` to lock SM clocks at 1410 MHz and eliminate DVFS frequency scaling jitter during benchmarks; reset to dynamic auto-scaling with `sudo nvidia-smi -rgc` when testing concludes.

## 7. Self-Assessment (What I Am Least Sure About)

1. **Bitmask variations across driver branches:** The standard bit positions for `clocks_throttle_reasons.active` (e.g. `0x20` for SW thermal and `0x80` for HW power brake) are defined in NVML, but exact bit names in XML versus CSV query outputs can vary slightly between major driver versions (e.g. 535 vs 550).
2. **PyTorch caching allocator internal defragmentation latency:** Compaction pauses during extreme memory pressure typically take hundreds of milliseconds, but exact pause time depends on tensor count and CUDA allocator configuration (`PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF=expandable_segments:True`).
3. **Power brake activation signals in OEM chassis:** External power brake slowdown (`0x80`) can be triggered by facility emergency power-off signals, PSU failure events, or chassis CPLD thermal alerts; exact wiring differs between DGX and OEM baseboards.
