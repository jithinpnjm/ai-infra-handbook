## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (`N = 1,000,003` indexing & coverage):** (a) `idx = 3 * 256 + 5 = 773`; (b) ceiling division `(1,000,003 + 255) // 256 = 3,907` blocks = `1,000,192` threads with `189` guarded extra threads; (c) truncated division `1,000,003 // 256 = 3,906` blocks = `999,936` threads, leaving `67` tail elements untouched at `0.0`.
2. **Exercise 2 (Warp lane waste):** (a) `<<<1, 50>>>` allocates `2` warps (`64` lanes) and leaves `64 - 50 = 14` lanes idle (`78.12%` active); (b) on `3840 × 2160`, `dim3(1, 1)` launches `8,294,400` blocks with `31` of `32` lanes idle per block (`96.88%` ~ `97%` waste), whereas `dim3(16, 16)` launches `240 × 135 = 32,400` blocks of `256` threads (`8` full warps) with `0%` lane waste.
3. **Exercise 3 (SM residency & grid underfill):** Blackwell allows at most `64` resident warps (`2,048` threads) and `32` active blocks per SM (`8` blocks of `256` threads or `2` blocks of `1,024` threads per SM); launching `<<<64, 256>>>` on an `H100` (`132` SMs) leaves `132 - 64 = 68` SMs completely idle.
4. **Exercise 4 (Transfer ratio & `cudaMalloc` overhead):** (a) In Lab 02, total transfer time is `0.847 + 0.412 = 1.259 ms` vs `0.096 ms` kernel = `13.11x` (`~13x`) transfer-to-kernel ratio; (b) `cudaMalloc` (`71.2%`) + `cudaFree` (`6.4%`) = `77.6%` of API time (`20.42x` the `3.8%` `cudaLaunchKernel` time), replaced by `cudaMallocAsync(&d_buf, bytes, stream)` and `cudaFreeAsync(d_buf, stream)`.
5. **Exercise 5 (Unified Memory behavior):** Cold Iteration 1 (`812.4 ms`) is `19.72x` (`~20x`) slower than warm Iteration 2 (`41.2 ms`), eliminated by `cudaMemPrefetchAsync` (`38.9 ms`); the CPU debug print (`96.7 ms`) is `23.59x` the `4.1 ms` kernel; crossing from `36 GB` (`104 GB/s`) to `44 GB` (`9 GB/s`) on a `40 GB` GPU drops throughput by `11.56x` due to page-eviction thrashing.
6. **Exercise 6 (Error bisection & inter-block deadlock):** `cudaMemcpy` is simply the first synchronizing call after `kernel_a` or `kernel_b` faulted asynchronously; insert `CUDA_CHECK(cudaDeviceSynchronize())` after `kernel_a` and `kernel_b` to pinpoint which kernel faulted, and split `Block 100` waiting on `Block 1` into two sequential kernel launches so spinning blocks cannot starve `Block 1` off the SMs.

## 5. Self-Assessment (What I Am Least Sure About)

1. **`N = 1,000,000` vs `N = 1,000,003` across sources:** *ASP* Ch 6 (p. 186) and `V3 chapter-chapter-04` (§3) use `N = 1,000,000` (`3,907` blocks = `1,000,192` threads, `192` extra threads), whereas `V3 Ch 4` (Fig. 3.4.1 & p. 319) and `V3 Lab 02` use `N = 1,000,003` (`3,907` blocks = `1,000,192` threads, `189` extra threads, `67` truncated). Both exact calculations are included and verified in the lab.
2. **`cudaMemPrefetchAsync` signature across CUDA 12 vs CUDA 13:** As noted in `V3 Ch 9` (line 142), newer CUDA toolkits replace the plain integer `dstDevice` argument in `cudaMemPrefetchAsync` with a `cudaMemLocation` struct; this is flagged in Section 3.3.
3. **`PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF` vs `PYTORCH_ALLOC_CONF`:** *ASP* Ch 6 (p. 193) notes that newer PyTorch releases alias/rename `PYTORCH_CUDA_ALLOC_CONF` to `PYTORCH_ALLOC_CONF`; both names are given in Section 3.2.
