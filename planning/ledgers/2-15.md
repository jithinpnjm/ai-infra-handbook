## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (Autonomous Systems, IGP vs. EGP Goals, and BGP Session Establishment over `TCP/179`):** (a) An IGP (`OSPF`, `IS-IS`) optimizes for fast shortest-path forwarding inside a single organization, whereas BGP (`RFC 4271`, an EGP) prioritizes **policy control** across Autonomous Systems (`16`-bit private `64512–65534`, `32`-bit private `4200000000–4294967294`); (b) running over **TCP port 179** gives BGP built-in reliable delivery, flow control, PMTUD, and 2-way connectivity, and in the BGP FSM (`Idle -> Connect -> Active -> OpenSent -> OpenConfirm -> Established`), **`Active` means the TCP connection failed and is retrying**, not that the session is up.
2. **Exercise 2 (BGP Path Attributes, `AS_PATH` Loop Detection, and Outbound vs. Inbound Traffic Steering):** The three well-known mandatory attributes are `ORIGIN` (`i < e < ?`), `AS_PATH`, and `NEXT_HOP`; a router rejects any eBGP update whose `AS_PATH` already contains its own ASN (`check_as_path_loop()`, overridden only by `allowas-in`); to steer **outbound** traffic (how packets leave your AS) set **`LOCAL_PREF`** inbound (higher wins, default `100`), and to influence **inbound** traffic (how remote ASes enter your AS) use **`AS_PATH` prepending**, **`MED`** (lower wins within the same neighbor AS), or **communities**.
3. **Exercise 3 (The BGP Best-Path Decision Process and `multipath-relax`):** Using `select_bgp_best_path()`, trace the core tie-breakers: (1) highest `WEIGHT` (local), (2) highest **`LOCAL_PREF`**, (3) locally originated, (4) shortest **`AS_PATH`**, (5) lowest `ORIGIN`, (6) lowest **`MED`**, (7) **`eBGP` over `iBGP`**, (8) lowest **IGP cost to `NEXT_HOP`** (hot-potato), and (9) oldest eBGP / lowest peer Router ID; in data-center eBGP fabrics, `bgp bestpath as-path multipath-relax` allows ECMP across equal-length paths with different spine/leaf ASNs.
4. **Exercise 4 (eBGP vs. iBGP Rules, `next-hop-self`, and Route Reflectors `RFC 4456`):** Because iBGP does not prepend to `AS_PATH`, an iBGP-learned route is **never re-advertised to another iBGP peer** (`evaluate_bgp_advertisement_rule()`), requiring either an `n(n - 1) / 2` full mesh (`4,950` sessions for `100` routers) or **Route Reflectors** (`197` sessions with `2` RRs, `compute_ibgp_sessions_full_mesh_vs_rr()`) using **`ORIGINATOR_ID`** and **`CLUSTER_LIST`** for loop prevention, plus **`next-hop-self`** so internal routers can reach the eBGP border router.
5. **Exercise 5 (Control-Plane Policy: Hot-Potato vs. Cold-Potato, Overlays, and Elephant vs. Mouse Flow Pinning, `CN` Chapter 17):** **Hot-potato routing** exits to the nearest peer (lowest IGP cost to `NEXT_HOP`), while **cold-potato routing** carries traffic across your own backbone (`LOCAL_PREF` / `MED`); on a `100 Mb/s` link (`CN` Ch. 17), a `9000`-byte elephant packet takes **`0.72 ms`** to serialize (`1500` B = **`0.12 ms`**) compared to **`0.05 ms`** for a `64`-byte mouse packet—queuing mouse packets behind elephant bursts adds `> 14x` serialization jitter, motivating QoS queuing, flow pinning, or adaptive routing.
6. **Exercise 6 (Securing and Operating BGP: `TCP-AO`/`MD5`, `GTSM`, Prefix Filtering, `maximum-prefix`, `RPKI`, and Graceful Shutdown):** Authenticate sessions with `TCP-AO` (`RFC 5925`) or MD5, enforce **GTSM (`ttl-security hops 1`, TTL `>= 254`)**, apply strict inbound/outbound `prefix-list` and `as-path` filters (`bgp ebgp-requires-policy`), cap routes with `maximum-prefix`, validate origin ASNs with **RPKI ROV (`RFC 6811`)**, and drain nodes cleanly before maintenance with the **`GRACEFUL_SHUTDOWN` community (`65535:0`)**.
