## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (Purpose First, Combinatorial Explosion, and *How* vs. *What* vs. *Accurate* Models, `CN` Chapter 24):** (a) A system is working only if it fulfills its **end-to-end application purpose** (e.g., `AllReduce` reaching `>= 90%` line rate, not merely \"all links up and BGP Established\", as illustrated by the wind-speed cable capacitive crosstalk story); (b) **How models** capture protocol theory/mechanisms, **What models** capture the baseline state of *your* specific network (keeping in mind tool blind spots, like a DC ohmmeter reading an inductor as a short in the storm-radar story), and **Accurate models** are those fit to the question while navigating up and down the **model tree**.
2. **Exercise 2 (Half-Split Binary Search, Manipulation for Causation, Simplification, and Immediate vs. Permanent Fixes):** Using `run_half_split_path_search()` along `["src_host", "leaf_a", "spine", "leaf_b", "dst_host"]`: test the signal at the midpoint (`spine`) first—if valid at `spine`, bisect downstream toward `dst_host` (`leaf_b`); if broken at `spine`, bisect upstream toward `src_host` (`leaf_a`); use controlled **manipulation** (changing 1 variable while holding others constant) to prove causation over correlation, **simplify** to the minimal failing set, and track every immediate workaround as **technical debt** until the permanent fix and blameless review complete.
3. **Exercise 3 (The Read-Only `netdiag.sh` Evidence Kit and Connectivity / Performance Decision Trees):** Capture volatile kernel, NIC, routing, socket, conntrack, and RDMA/GPU state **before** rebooting or restarting services (`netdiag.sh`), and walk the Section 6 connectivity decision tree (`only A vs all clients` -> `ss -tlnp on B` -> `RST vs timeout` -> `half-split SYN/SYN-ACK capture`).
4. **Exercise 4 (Diagnosing Host and NAT Case Studies 1–5 and 7):** Using `classify_case_study_symptom()`: Case 1 is **SNAT port exhaustion** (`~60,000` ports per `(src_ip, dst_ip, dst_port)`); Case 2 is a **PMTU black hole** (blocked ICMP `3/4`); Case 3 is **`nf_conntrack` table full** (`5`-day default TCP timeout); Case 4 is **multi-NIC ARP flux + strict `rp_filter=1`**; Case 5 is **UDP receive buffer overflow** (`UdpRcvbufErrors`); and Case 7 is a **TX checksum offload bug** (verified by bad checksums arriving at the *receiver*, not the sender).
5. **Exercise 5 (Diagnosing Routing, Container, and Transport Case Studies 6 and 9–13):** Exact `5`-minute repeating outages (Case 6) point to **BGP route-flap dampening** triggered by a failing optic; `15 Gb/s` Pod throughput with 1 core at 100% `softirq` (Case 9) is the **`veth` bottleneck**; `TCP Window Size: 0` (Case 10) is a **slow receiving application**; host-BGP TCP degradation (Case 11) is **asymmetric ECMP reordering**; Case 12 is **missing L2 multicast in VXLAN overlays**; and exact `5 s` Kubernetes lookup stalls (Case 13) are the **conntrack UDP `A`/`AAAA` race + `ndots:5`**.
6. **Exercise 6 (Diagnosing AI-Fabric Case Studies 8 and 14: `PFC` Storms and Marginal Optics / Slow Rails):** In Case 8, millions of `rx_pause` frames per second collapse `NCCL` throughput because switch **ECN thresholds were set above PFC `XOFF`** (so ECN never slowed senders before hard PFC pauses froze the fabric); in Case 14, a single dirty/failing optical transceiver with low DOM `Rx power` and rising **pre-FEC corrected bits / CRC errors** causes retransmissions on one rail, making all 4,000 GPUs wait at every synchronous collective barrier.
