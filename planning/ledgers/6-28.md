## 4. One-Line Expected Exercise Solutions

1. **Exercise 1 (Statistical Benchmarking and Heterogeneous-Core Stragglers):** Warm-up eliminates first-call page faults and thread-pool setup; the scaled residual $< 16$ verifies numerical accuracy; and 8 threads on a 4P+4E Apple M3 ran $4\times$ slower (`22.9` vs `90.8 GFLOP/s`) because static LU partitioning forces fast P-cores to wait for slow E-cores at every barrier.
2. **Exercise 2 (Calculating `nccl-tests` Algorithm Bandwidth and Bus Bandwidth):** For `8 GiB` (`8.59 GB`) in `0.60 s` across `64` ranks, `algbw = 8.59 / 0.60 = 14.3 GB/s` and `AllReduce` `busbw = 14.3 * 2 * (63/64) = 28.2 GB/s` (`56%` of a `50 GB/s` NDR IB rail; check `NCCL_DEBUG=INFO` transport, GPU-NIC NUMA/GDR affinity, rail routing, and pairwise `osu_bw`).
3. **Exercise 3 (Active Benchmarking and Debunking Cached Benchmarks):** `iostat`, `bpftrace`, `cachestat`, and `strace` proved `bonnie++`'s `739 KB/s` `Per Chr` test wrote 1-byte buffers to the page cache with 0 disk IOPS; similarly, `50,000 IOPS * 8 KB = 400,000 KB/s = 3.2 Gbps` exceeds a `1 Gbps` link (`119–120 MB/s`), proving client caching.
4. **Exercise 4 (Sizing HPL `HPL.dat` and Contrasting HPL, HPCG, and STREAM):** Size $N = \sqrt{0.85 \times \text{Bytes} / 8}$ (`80–90%` of RAM) with a near-square grid $P \times Q$ ($8 \times 8$); HPL measures dense FP64 compute (`60–90%` `Rpeak`), HPCG measures sparse memory/interconnect performance (`< 5%` `Rpeak`), and STREAM measures DRAM/HBM Triad bandwidth (`80–90%` of bus peak).
5. **Exercise 5 (Benchmarking Parallel Storage `IOR`, `mdtest`, and `gdsio`):** Defeat client caching in `IOR` with `-C` rank reordering, datasets $> 2\times$ client RAM, and cache drops; use `gdsio` `-x 0` vs `-x 2` to verify GPUDirect Storage DMA (`9.6` vs `8.0 GB/s`, `25%` vs `85%` CPU).
6. **Exercise 6 (Distinguishing Communication-Bound vs Compute-Bound Training):** Halving the micro-batch size halves compute while keeping DDP gradient `all_reduce` bytes constant; if NIC throughput stays pinned at `60 GB/s` the job is communication-bound, whereas if it drops to `40 GB/s` the job is compute-bound.
