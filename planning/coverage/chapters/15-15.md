Why CPU, Memory, and GPU SM Utilization Fail as LLM Autoscaling Signals** | **CPU & Memory Decoupling:** In vLLM, SGLang, TGI, and TensorRT-LLM, host CPU only handles HTTP/gRPC ingress, tokenization (`~1.4 ms` in Rust), and async scheduling while the forward pass executes on the GPU; host RAM and VRAM are statically pre-allocated at startup (`--gpu-memory-utilization 0.90`, e.g., `79.14 GiB * 0.90 = 71.22 GiB` on an 80 GB GPU with `14.99 GiB` weights, `0.12 GiB` non-torch, `1.19 GiB` activation peak, and `54.93 GiB` PagedAttention KV pool). Thus memory usage is a flat line (`90%`) from `0` to `256` active requests. **Why `DCGM_FI_DEV_GPU_UTIL` Fails:** `DCGM_FI_DEV_GPU_UTIL` measures the percentage of sample intervals during which *at least one* CUDA kernel was active on the GPU—not how full the batch or SMs are. Because continuous batching runs an autoregressive decode loop (`18–42 ms`/step) for *even a single active user* (`batch=1`), `DCGM_FI_DEV_GPU_UTIL` jumps from `0%` to `98%–100%` at `1` request and stays at `100%` up to `128` requests (`LLP` Ch. 6 notes individual GPU utilization oscillates between `0%` and `100%`, causing violent HPA flapping).
