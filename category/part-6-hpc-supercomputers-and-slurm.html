Part 6: HPC, supercomputers and Slurm

31 chapters. Default priority tier 1.

📄️6.1 What a supercomputer is: TOP500, Linpack, HPC versus AI factory

What makes a computer super: parallelism, FLOPS and precision, Rpeak versus Rmax, the TOP500, HPL, HPCG and Green500 lists, how traditional HPC differs from an AI factory, cluster types, and what the word means to the administrator.

📄️6.2 Cluster anatomy: head, login, compute, storage and management nodes

The parts of an HPC or GPU cluster and the job each does: head and control nodes, login nodes, compute nodes, service nodes, storage, the four networks, facility infrastructure, planning and documentation, and the life of a user session and a job.

📄️6.3 Parallel computing concepts: Amdahl, scaling, shared memory versus message passing

The ideas behind parallel programs: speedup and efficiency, Amdahl and Gustafson, strong and weak scaling, Karp-Flatt, shared memory versus message passing versus PGAS versus accelerators, communication cost models, load balance, and the software landscape.

📄️6.4 MPI in practice: Open MPI, ranks, collectives and tuning

Using MPI on a cluster: ranks and communicators, point-to-point and collective operations, thread levels, Open MPI architecture, launching with mpirun and srun, mapping and binding, tuning, CUDA-aware MPI, benchmarking, and diagnosing hangs.

📄️6.5 OpenMP, compilers and optimisation flags

Node-level parallelism and compilation for HPC: the OpenMP model, directives, data scoping, schedules, affinity and first-touch, common bugs, compiler families, optimisation and floating-point flags, vectorisation reports, portability, and accelerator directives.

📄️6.6 Environment modules, Spack and EasyBuild

Managing scientific and AI software on shared clusters: how environment variables select software, Environment Modules and Lmod, Spack, EasyBuild, containers and conda as alternatives, site layout, GPU toolchains, and operational practice.

📄️6.7 Slurm architecture and installation

How Slurm is built (slurmctld, slurmd, slurmstepd, slurmdbd, munge, plugins), what happens to a job from submit to accounting, and how to install and verify a working cluster.

📄️6.8 Slurm configuration: nodes, partitions and cgroups

Configuring Slurm for a GPU cluster: node definitions and states, features, weights and reserved resources, partitions and their limits, resource selection, cgroup containment, topology awareness, and validation.

📄️6.9 Slurm accounting and scheduling policy: QOS, fair-share, backfill, preemption

How Slurm decides who runs next and how much anyone may use: accounting associations and TRES, limits and QOS, the multifactor priority formula, Fair Tree fair-share with a worked simulator, usage decay, backfill and why time limits matter, preemption modes, and policy designs for AI clusters

📄️6.10 Slurm GPU scheduling: GRES, NVML autodetect, MIG and device constraints

Making Slurm schedule GPUs correctly: GRES configuration (slurm.conf, gres.conf, AutoDetect), types, topology (Cores and Links), the request options (--gres, --gpus*, --cpus-per-gpu, --mem-per-gpu, --gpu-bind, --gres-flags), device isolation, MIG, MPS and sharding, accounting for GPUs, and debugging

📄️6.11 Submitting and launching jobs: sbatch, srun, salloc, arrays, dependencies

How users run work on Slurm: batch scripts and directives, allocations versus steps, srun inside and outside allocations, interactive jobs, job arrays, dependencies, signals and requeue, output files, environment variables, monitoring and cancelling, and the patterns for AI training jobs, with a linter for batch scripts

📄️6.12 Multi-node training on Slurm: PMIx, torchrun, NCCL environment, binding

Launching distributed training under Slurm: control plane versus data plane, srun with one task per GPU versus torchrun per node, rendezvous from Slurm variables (tested with a fake-srun DDP harness), PMIx for MPI, NCCL environment variables, CPU/GPU/NIC binding, FSDP/DeepSpeed/Megatron launch patterns, and diagnosing hangs

📄️6.13 Containers on Slurm: enroot and pyxis

Running containerised GPU jobs under Slurm with NVIDIA enroot and the pyxis SPANK plugin: how they differ from Docker, image import and squashfs, the srun --container-* options, mounts, hooks for GPUs and InfiniBand, registry credentials, caching, PMIx and PyTorch integration, security model, and operating image distribution at scale

📄️6.14 Slurm operations: prolog, epilog, health checks, drain, reason codes, troubleshooting

Day-two Slurm: prolog and epilog semantics and failure behaviour, health-check programs and automatic draining, node lifecycle (drain, resume, down, reboot), unkillable steps, reading logs and sdiag, controller load, a tested example health-check script, and a troubleshooting playbook for stuck, failed and slow jobs

📄️6.15 Slurm high availability, upgrades and scaling

Keeping Slurm running and current: controller and database failover, state storage, backups, the documented upgrade order and compatibility window (with a path planner), reverting, scaling to thousands of nodes and high job rates (sysctls, timers, select plugin, tree fan-out, munge), configless and sackd, REST and authentication, federation and elastic nodes

📄️6.16 Slurm on Kubernetes: Slinky and Soperator

Running Slurm clusters on Kubernetes: why, the two open-source approaches (Slinky slurm-operator and Nebius Soperator) with their CRDs and design, drain/cordon coordination, shared root (jail), active health checks, ephemeral nodes, and how to choose between bare-metal Slurm, Slurm on Kubernetes, and native Kubernetes batch (Kueue)

📄️6.17 Other schedulers: PBS, Torque, LSF and Flux

The other batch schedulers you will meet: OpenPBS/PBS Professional and the legacy Torque, IBM Spectrum LSF, and Flux (El Capitan's workload manager): architecture, request syntax, a command map against Slurm, a tested PBS-to-Slurm directive converter, and guidance for migrations and mixed environments

📄️6.18 Parallel storage concepts and the limits of NFS

Why clusters need shared storage and why a single NFS server stops scaling: NFS semantics and tuning, metadata versus data paths, striping, locking and caching, N-to-1 versus N-to-N I/O, small-file problems, checkpoint bandwidth arithmetic, the landscape of parallel file systems, time synchronisation, and a micro-benchmark with measured numbers

📄️6.19 Lustre architecture and operation

Lustre in depth: MGS/MDS/MDT/OSS/OST/clients and LNet, creating a file system, striping (raid0, PFL composite layouts, DoM), directory striping (DNE), quotas and project quotas, pools, migration, failover, monitoring and tuning, and diagnosing metadata and data bottlenecks for AI workloads, with a striping model and corrections to common misconceptions

📄️6.20 BeeGFS, GPFS, WekaFS and VAST

The other parallel and scale-out file systems used on GPU clusters: BeeGFS (services, config, RDMA, BeeOND), IBM Storage Scale/GPFS, PanFS, WEKA and VAST, with architecture, strengths, limits, selection criteria, and a comparison with Lustre; claims checked against BeeGFS sources where possible

📄️6.21 Parallel I/O and checkpoint patterns

How parallel programs do I/O and how training jobs checkpoint: MPI-IO and HDF5 collective I/O, file-per-process versus shared file versus sharded checkpoints (with a tested DCP resharding demo), asynchronous and tiered checkpointing, a tested crash-safe commit protocol, restart and validation, data-loading pipelines, and how stripe alignment and metadata shape performance

📄️6.22 Provisioning with PXE, iPXE, DHCP, TFTP and image management

How cluster nodes get an operating system: the boot chain (firmware, bootloader, kernel, initramfs, init), DHCP/TFTP/PXE, iPXE and UEFI HTTP boot, stateless and diskless images, NFS-root and squashfs, image building and versioning, node identity and discovery, a tested inventory-to-dnsmasq/iPXE generator, and a boot-failure triage method

📄️6.23 xCAT, Warewulf and cluster managers compared with BCM

Cluster management systems compared: xCAT (architecture, object model, key commands, current project status), Warewulf 4 (nodes.conf, images, overlays, wwctl), Confluent, Canonical MAAS and Foreman, and NVIDIA Base Command Manager (head node, software images, categories, cmsh), with a selection matrix for GPU clusters

📄️6.24 Users, LDAP, SSSD, quotas and accounting at scale

Identity and accounting for clusters: consistent UIDs/GIDs, NIS/NIS+ history, LDAP schema and operation, SSSD and nsswitch/PAM configuration, SSH keys and MFA, home directory provisioning, storage and CPU quotas, Slurm account synchronisation, onboarding and offboarding, scaling and security, with a tested generator that derives LDIF, Slurm accounts and home setup from one validated user list

📄️6.25 Remote access, bastions, Open OnDemand and user environments

How users reach and use a cluster: SSH (login nodes, bastions, jump hosts, keys, certificates, MFA, hardening with a tested sshd audit), X11 and port forwarding, Open OnDemand architecture and cluster configuration, Jupyter and VS Code access, shells and dotfiles, quotas and etiquette, and the human side: tickets, documentation, onboarding and education

📄️6.26 Cluster monitoring and health at scale

Monitoring a GPU cluster: from SNMP, Ganglia, Nagios and Zabbix to Prometheus/Grafana, what to measure (node, GPU via DCGM, fabric, storage, Slurm, facility), exporters, alert design and runbooks, health checks and security scans, XDMoD-style usage analytics; with a tested Slurm exporter and an alert-rule checker validated against dcgm-exporter's metric list

📄️6.27 Backup and data protection

Backup and data protection for GPU clusters: what to protect and what not to, RPO/RTO and the 3-2-1 rule, tar, rsync snapshots, Bacula, modern deduplicating tools (restic, borg, kopia), snapshots and replication, parallel file system backup challenges, ransomware and immutability, and restore testing, with tested rsync, GNU tar and restic experiments

📄️6.28 HPC benchmarking: HPL, HPCG, IOR, OSU and NCCL tests

Benchmarking a cluster properly: methodology (active benchmarking, USE, sanity checks, statistics, failure modes), the standard benchmarks (STREAM, HPL, HPCG, IOR/mdtest, OSU micro-benchmarks, NCCL tests with bus-bandwidth formulas, MLPerf), acceptance testing and burn-in, with a tested statistics harness that exposes a 4x thread-count surprise on a heterogeneous-core CPU

📄️6.29 Containers and reproducibility in HPC: Apptainer and enroot

Running containers on HPC and GPU clusters reproducibly: Apptainer/Singularity (SIF, definition files, build, GPU --nv, MPI hybrid and bind models, cache, signing), Charliecloud, enroot compared, security models, and a reproducibility discipline (pinning by digest, lock files, labels, tests, SBOMs) backed by a tested definition-file and Dockerfile linter

📄️6.30 Facility basics: power, cooling, racks and liquid cooling

The physical side of a GPU cluster explained from scratch: how much power servers and switches use, how power reaches a rack (circuits, breakers, redundancy), how heat is removed (air, containment, liquid), racks, cables, UPS and safe shutdown, PUE and energy cost, with calculators that reproduce NVIDIA's published rack tables

📄️6.31 Capstone: build and operate a small Slurm GPU cluster

Bring all of Part 6 together: plan, build, test, and operate a small Slurm GPU cluster from a single hardware inventory using the Part 6 toolchain pipeline, then master senior architecture whiteboard scenarios covering DGX SuperPOD, Base Command Manager, 64-node hang triage, and Slurm versus Kubernetes coexistence
